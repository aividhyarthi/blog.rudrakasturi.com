## Editorial template used

The drafts now follow the structure of the published [OpenAI ChatGPT Ads partner app article](https://blog.rudrakasturi.com/openai-chatgpt-ads-partner-app-access-default-december-9-2026/):

- Standard front matter: title, date, description, tag, tldr, categories, tags, type and sources.
- Opening paragraphs that explain the news in plain English.
- A relevant image close to the top, with an image credit or source link directly below it.
- Clear H2 sections that explain what happened, why it matters and what changes.
- Practical implications, actions or a measurement framework.
- A substantial FAQ section using natural search questions.
- A closing takeaway and an editorial note that distinguishes confirmed facts from analysis.
- Sources are listed in the site's source box from front matter.

## Images and source handling

Each article has an original 1600 × 900 SVG illustration in `public/images/editorial/`. The Markdown embeds use stable raw GitHub image URLs so they display in GitHub previews as well as the published site. The Google article also includes an image linked directly from Google's official announcement, with its source credited immediately below it. For the other stories, the source document is linked immediately below the original editorial illustration; no official image asset has been embedded unless a stable image URL was available and checked.

- The Google AI Overviews article is a general news explainer, not a publisher-only article. It explains AEO, brand recall, traffic, clicks, returning visitors, brand investment and a 90-day action plan. Publisher and e-commerce follow-ups can be separate articles.

- The ARTEX article does not claim a wider victim breach; it preserves JFrog's qualification that no separate victim data was confirmed.
- Every article includes FAQs and at least two relevant internal links.
