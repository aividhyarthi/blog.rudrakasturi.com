_AI + human review is the new formula for multilingual SEO success_

### What Changed?

Google recently updated its guidance on automated translations, moving away from a strict "automated = spam" stance to a more nuanced view: machine translations are acceptable for indexing if the content is high quality and user-friendly.

**John Mueller of Google Search Relations confirmed:**

> _**“If the pages are well‑translated… if you think they’re good for your users, then making them indexable is fine.”**_

### Real-World Examples: What Works vs. What Fails

Scenario

Outcome

Why

A travel website uses DeepL to translate their Italy guide to German, then has a native speaker refine the content.

Indexed successfully

Human review catches idioms, local nuance, and cultural phrasing.

A blog uses Google Translate to auto-translate 500 articles into French and publishes them without review.

Penalized or deindexed

Phrases come out awkward or misleading; some technical terms don’t translate accurately.

An e-commerce store uses AI to translate product descriptions, then edits the top 100 SKUs manually.

Performs well in search

Prioritization plus hybrid editing ensures scalability and accuracy.

### No Need for a “Machine Translation” Tag

Google doesn't require any special schema or markup to flag translated content. Instead, you should:

*   Use `noindex` for low-quality pages
*   Let high-quality translations be indexed normally
*   Focus on helpful, natural, and localized content

### Best Practices for Using AI Translation Responsibly

1.  **Use AI for scale, not shortcuts**  
    Start with DeepL, Google Translate, or GPT models but don’t publish without review.
2.  **Always involve a human editor**  
    Even a quick review by a native speaker can prevent awkward or inaccurate phrasing.  
    **_Example: "I want to break fast" is not the same as "I want breakfast."_**
3.  **Check cultural and contextual accuracy**  
    Literal translations of regional idioms often fail.  
    **_Example: "Ghar ki murgi daal barabar" won’t work in German better to use "Familiarity breeds contempt" or its local equivalent._**
4.  **Use `hreflang` tags**  
    Ensure Google serves the right language version to the right audience.
5.  **Prioritize pages by traffic or ROI**  
    Focus on your highest-performing blogs or product listings first.

### Google’s message is clear:

Machine-translated content is fine as long as it's reviewed, refined, and valuable to users.

This creates new possibilities for:

*   Publishers expanding into new regions
*   E-commerce platforms targeting international buyers
*   Travel, finance, education, and healthcare content going multilingual

But success depends on quality and effort. Automation is a tool — not a free pass.
