For publishers, one of the biggest questions around AI search is not whether an article can be cited. It is whether a person will notice the citation and visit the website.

Google's Dive Deeper feature is designed to make that next step more visible. The feature presents related articles after an AI Mode response, using more prominent links and image thumbnails to encourage users to explore specific angles of a topic.

Google announced the design direction in October. On November 23, Search Engine Land and Search Engine Roundtable reported that the feature was appearing in AI Mode. That makes this a rollout story rather than a completely new product announcement.

The change is worth watching. But publishers should be careful about calling it a traffic recovery before referral data supports that conclusion.

## What is Google AI Mode's Dive Deeper feature?

AI Mode gives users an AI-generated response to a query and allows them to continue exploring through follow-up questions. Dive Deeper adds a more visible set of related article links after some responses.

Google's October announcement said it wanted to make links at the end of some AI responses more engaging and prominent, with image thumbnails and a more clickable design. The idea is to help users move from an initial overview to articles that offer additional reporting or a different angle.

The feature is different from a conventional list of blue links. The AI response comes first. The related publisher links appear later, after the user has received the generated answer.

That position is central to understanding both the opportunity and the limitation.

## What changed on November 23?

Search Engine Land reported that Dive Deeper was appearing in AI Mode after being spotted in the rollout over the preceding weekend. Search Engine Roundtable also reported seeing the feature live.

The observed presentation includes prominent blue links, thumbnails and short descriptions. The links are intended to make related articles easier to identify and open.

The important detail is that users may need to scroll past a substantial AI-generated response before reaching the section. A more visible link is useful, but visibility depends on where it appears in the experience and how much attention the answer above it consumes.

The feature's availability and presentation may vary during rollout. Publishers should test it across different queries, devices and locations rather than assume every user sees the same layout.

## Does this solve the publisher traffic problem?

Not by itself.

A link can be visually prominent and still receive few clicks if users feel that the AI response has already answered their question. A user who wants a quick definition may have no reason to continue. Someone researching a complex purchase, policy or technical issue may be more likely to open a deeper article.

This suggests that the feature may be more useful for topics where the next step is genuinely valuable: original reporting, expert analysis, product comparisons, investigations, detailed explainers and specialist advice.

It does not establish that all publishers will receive more visits. Google has not published a traffic uplift attributable to Dive Deeper in the rollout coverage.

## What kind of articles should publishers prepare?

The feature reinforces the value of articles that offer a clear next step beyond the summary.

**Original reporting:** A news report with verified facts, named sources and additional context gives the reader something to inspect beyond a generated overview.

**Evidence-led analysis:** An article that explains why a development matters, compares competing explanations or analyses a dataset can attract readers who need more than a short answer.

**Practical guides:** Detailed instructions, worked examples and decision frameworks are useful when a person needs to complete a task.

**Specialist perspectives:** A clear argument from an experienced practitioner can help readers understand trade-offs that a general answer may flatten.

**Local context:** Indian readers may need state-specific, industry-specific or language-specific information that a broad answer does not fully address.

This is not a reason to make every article longer. It is a reason to make the additional value obvious.

## A measurement plan for publishers

Newsrooms and SEO teams should create a small baseline before making claims about the feature.

1. Select a fixed group of queries where the site has relevant articles.
2. Check whether AI Mode displays a Dive Deeper section for those queries.
3. Record which publishers and URLs appear, the article topic and the position of the section.
4. Repeat the checks across mobile and desktop where possible.
5. Track referral sessions from Google AI Mode where analytics can identify them.
6. Compare landing-page engagement and conversions, not just visits.
7. Review the same queries over several weeks because a rollout can change.

Keep the observations separate from standard organic search reporting. A link appearing in an AI Mode experience should not automatically be counted as a normal organic ranking or impression.

Also record cases where a site is cited inside the AI response but does not appear in Dive Deeper. These are different forms of visibility and may have different click behaviour.

## What Indian publishers should watch

The feature could matter to Indian publishers covering technology, finance, education, health, public policy and consumer decisions. These topics often involve more than one simple answer.

A reader may accept an AI summary of a policy change but still want to open the original government notification. A person comparing financial products may need the detailed terms and conditions. A reader following a technology launch may want independent testing rather than a short summary.

Publishers should make the value of the article clear in its headline, introduction and reporting. The page should answer the immediate question while offering evidence, context and practical detail that justify a visit.

There is no confirmed India-specific click uplift in the rollout reports. Local performance needs to be measured rather than assumed.

## The takeaway

Google's Dive Deeper rollout is a useful development because it gives related publisher articles a more visible place after some AI Mode responses. It recognises that users may want to go beyond an AI-generated overview.

But a better-looking link is not the same as a meaningful referral. The feature appears after the generated answer, and users may have to scroll to reach it.

Publishers should treat Dive Deeper as a new discovery surface to monitor, not as a replacement for lost search traffic. The strongest opportunity lies in publishing material that gives readers a genuine reason to continue: original reporting, useful evidence, specialist judgement and clear next steps.

The real test will be whether these links send qualified readers to publisher websites, and whether those readers stay, subscribe or return.
