For publishers, the question around AI search is increasingly direct: when Google answers a query inside AI Mode, will the person still visit the publisher's website?

Google's Dive Deeper feature is designed to make links to related articles more visible after some AI Mode responses. The links appear with descriptions and image thumbnails, giving users another way to move from an AI-generated answer to a publisher's page.

Google outlined this direction in October. On November 23, Search Engine Land and Search Engine Roundtable reported that the feature was appearing in AI Mode. The change is worth watching because it creates another potential source of referral traffic. But a more visible link does not automatically mean more website visits.

## What are Google AI Mode's Dive Deeper links?

Google AI Mode generates an answer and allows users to continue with follow-up questions. Dive Deeper adds a section of related web articles after some responses.

Google's October announcement described a more engaging and prominent presentation for links at the end of AI responses, including image thumbnails. The aim is to help users discover pages that provide more detail, reporting or another perspective on the topic.

This is different from a traditional search results page. The AI-generated answer appears first, while the related publisher links are placed after it. That order matters because the user may already have received enough information without opening another page.

## What changed in the November 2026 rollout?

Search Engine Land reported on November 23 that Dive Deeper was appearing in AI Mode after being spotted during the preceding weekend. Search Engine Roundtable also reported seeing the feature live.

The observed design includes prominent links, short descriptions and thumbnails. These elements can make a publisher article easier to recognise than a plain text link.

However, the section appears after the generated response. Depending on the length of the answer, a user may need to scroll before reaching it. The rollout may also vary by query, device and location, so publishers should not assume every searcher sees the same layout.

## Could Dive Deeper increase traffic to publisher websites?

It could create an additional route to a publisher's website, but the actual traffic impact has not yet been established.

Consider two searches. Someone asking for a simple definition may read the AI response and leave. Someone researching a policy change, a financial decision or a new technology may want to open a detailed report, inspect the original evidence or compare expert views.

The second type of search is more likely to create a reason for a website visit. That is why the value of the linked article matters as much as the visual design of the link.

Google has not published a measured traffic uplift attributable to Dive Deeper in the rollout reports. Publishers should treat it as a potential referral surface, not as confirmed recovery of traffic lost to AI answers.

## Which publisher articles are more likely to earn a click?

Articles need to offer something beyond the AI-generated response.

Original reporting can give readers verified facts, named sources and details that are not present in a short summary. Evidence-led analysis can explain why a development matters and compare competing interpretations. Practical guides can help someone complete a task. Specialist articles can explain trade-offs that a general answer may leave out.

For example, a short AI answer about a new government policy may tell a reader what changed. A publisher article that links to the official notification, explains which groups are affected and gives state-level examples offers a stronger reason to visit.

This is not a reason to inflate article length. The additional value should be clear from the headline, opening paragraphs and reporting.

## How should publishers measure traffic from AI Mode?

SEO and audience teams should establish a baseline before claiming that Dive Deeper is increasing referrals.

1. Select a fixed set of queries where the publisher has relevant articles.
2. Check whether a Dive Deeper section appears for each query in AI Mode.
3. Record the publisher names, article URLs, link descriptions and the section's position.
4. Repeat the checks across mobile and desktop, and record the location and date.
5. Monitor identifiable Google AI Mode referral sessions in analytics where available.
6. Compare landing-page engagement, registrations, subscriptions and conversions, not just sessions.
7. Repeat the same checks over several weeks to separate a lasting pattern from rollout changes.

Keep this reporting separate from conventional organic rankings. A link shown inside AI Mode is not automatically equivalent to a standard blue-link ranking or a Search Console impression.

Also record when a publisher is cited inside the AI-generated answer but does not appear in Dive Deeper. A citation in the response and a related article link below it are different placements, and they may produce different click behaviour.

## What should Indian publishers do now?

Indian publishers covering technology, finance, education, health, public policy and consumer decisions should check whether their articles appear for relevant AI Mode prompts.

A reader may accept a short summary of a policy change but still want to inspect the original government notification. Someone comparing financial products may need detailed fees and eligibility conditions. A person following a technology launch may want independent testing rather than a summary of the announcement.

Make the article's value clear. Use a specific headline, explain the development early, link to original documents and include evidence, context and practical examples. Do not assume that adding an image or increasing word count will secure a link or a click.

There is no confirmed India-specific traffic uplift from Dive Deeper in the rollout reports. Publishers need to measure their own referrals.

## Questions about Google AI Mode Dive Deeper

### Is Google AI Mode Dive Deeper live?

The feature was announced by Google in October and was reported appearing in AI Mode on November 23, 2026. Its availability and presentation may vary during rollout.

### Do Dive Deeper links send traffic to publisher websites?

They provide links that users can open, so they can generate website visits. However, Google has not published a measured traffic increase attributable to the feature. Publishers should verify referral sessions in their own analytics.

### Is a Dive Deeper link the same as an AI citation?

Not necessarily. A source can be cited within an AI-generated response, while Dive Deeper presents related articles after the response. Track these placements separately.

### How can publishers improve their chances of earning traffic from AI Mode?

Publish original reporting, useful analysis, expert context and practical guides that add value beyond a short AI answer. Make the page accurate, easy to understand and supported by original sources. There is no guaranteed method to secure a Dive Deeper placement.

Dive Deeper gives publisher articles a more visible place within some AI Mode experiences. That is a useful development, but the outcome publishers care about is not the number of thumbnails shown. It is whether people click through, spend time with the reporting and return to the publication. Until referral data demonstrates that, the feature should be treated as a new discovery opportunity to measure, not a promise of traffic growth.
