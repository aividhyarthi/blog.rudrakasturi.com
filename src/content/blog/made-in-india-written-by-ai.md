I’m going to be honest.  
This article - the one you’re reading right now - is partly written by AI.

Not ghostwritten, not fully automated. Just... assisted.  
But even that feels strange.  
Because I’m using a machine to write about how machines are quietly replacing the human mind.

That’s where we’ve reached.  
A world where we need AI to explain what AI is doing to us.

### ****How It Started****

A few years ago, the internet was a living organism.  
You could taste people’s opinions - raw, emotional, imperfect, but real.  
Now, you taste formatting.

Oxford researchers recently found that almost **half of all content on the internet is AI-written**, and by next year, it will cross **90%**.

Nine out of ten words online will be generated, not felt.

And yet, here I am also generating words.  
Because time is short, attention is shorter, and even the best of us are quietly giving in to convenience.

![](https://blog.rudrakasturi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/chatgpt-799x1024.webp)

### ****The Silent Collapse****

Something happened after 2022.  
Every blog started sounding like a marketing brochure.  
Every “how-to” article felt like déjà vu.  
Even opinions started sounding neutral too polite to offend, too empty to inspire.

That’s because AI doesn’t think.  
It predicts.  
And when everyone starts relying on predictions, the future becomes predictable.

We have built machines that can write faster than thought, but not deeper than feeling.

### ****What’s Happening in India****

In India, AI content has exploded.  
Regional newsrooms, brand blogs, YouTube scripts, even political speeches - all being “co-written” by algorithms.

It’s efficient, no doubt.  
But here’s the price we’re paying  
we’re losing the _human accent_ of our own internet.

That raw Indian voice - the one that mixes sarcasm with sadness, pride with pain - is slowly getting replaced by a synthetic global tone that’s perfect, polished, and utterly lifeless.

### **Model Collapse, Or The Copy-Paste Apocalypse**

When AI starts learning from AI content, it begins a process researchers call **“model collapse.”**  
Imagine photocopying a photocopy until nothing’s left but faded outlines.

That’s where we’re heading.  
AI trains on the internet.  
The internet is written by AI.  
So now AI is learning from itself.

Every new generation will be a duller copy of the last.

### **And Yet... Here I Am**

I can’t pretend I’m innocent in this story.  
I used AI to structure this very article - to save time, to refine tone, to test clarity.

But after the first draft, I did what no machine could do.  
I paused.  
I rewrote.  
I questioned.

Because reflection isn’t automation.  
It’s resistance.

The truth is: AI didn’t take away human creativity.  
We abandoned it - willingly, for convenience.

### **The Real Takeaway**

The internet isn’t dying because of AI.  
It’s dying because of **our surrender**.

We stopped writing with intent.  
We stopped reading to understand.  
We outsourced curiosity.

AI didn’t replace us - it reflected us.  
And maybe that’s the part that hurts the most.

### **What We Can Still Do**

1.  **Use AI as a tool, not a crutch.**  
    Let it sharpen you, not silence you.
2.  **Protect human chaos.**  
    Keep the tone, the typos, the temper. Machines can’t fake that.
3.  **Don’t chase perfection.**  
    Perfect writing is predictable writing.
4.  **Remember the pause.**  
    Thinking is our last remaining luxury.

### **So yes… I used AI to write this.**

But the thoughts, the doubts, the discomfort - those are mine.  
And maybe that’s all that matters now.  
Not who types the words, but who _feels_ them.

Because when the web becomes a mirror of machines,  
the only way to stay human is to leave fingerprints.
