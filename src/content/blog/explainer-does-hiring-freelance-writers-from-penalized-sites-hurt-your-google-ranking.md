Google’s Search Liaison, Danny Sullivan, recently addressed a common concern: _If you hire freelancers who previously worked on sites hit by Google penalties (like spam or low-quality content), will your site get penalized too?_

Here’s a simple breakdown of what you need to know:

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/image-7-1024x768.png)

Pic Credit: @[@Jonny\_J](https://x.com/Jonny_J_)

#### **1\. Google Doesn’t Penalize Freelancers—It Judges Content**

*   **No “Guilt by Association”**: Just because a freelancer worked on a penalized site doesn’t mean Google will punish _your_ site for hiring them.
*   **Content Quality Matters**: Google cares about **what’s on your site**, not who wrote it. If the freelancer creates helpful, original content for you, there’s no risk.

#### **2\. There’s No “Safe Threshold” for Freelancers**

*   **No Magic Number**: Google won’t say, “You can hire up to 5 freelancers from penalized sites.” There’s no limit because the focus is **content quality**, not freelancer count.
*   **Example**: Imagine hiring 10 freelancers—if all their work is great, you’re fine. But even one freelancer producing spammy content could hurt you.

#### **3\. Google Loves Freelancers (If They Create Good Content)**

Danny Sullivan clarified:

*   **“We don’t hate freelancers”**: Google lists tons of content from sites that use freelancers.
*   **“We like you \[freelancers\]”**: Freelancers aren’t the problem—_low-quality content_ is.

#### **4\. How to Stay Safe**

*   **Focus on Quality**: Ensure freelancers write **original, useful content** that follows Google’s [E-E-A-T guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) (Expertise, Experience, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness).
*   **Avoid Red Flags**:
    *   Don’t copy content from penalized sites.
    *   Don’t ask freelancers to stuff keywords or create “thin” content (short, unhelpful articles).
*   **Edit and Review**: Always check freelancers’ work for accuracy and value.

#### **5\. What If the Freelancer’s Past Work Was Bad?**

*   **It Doesn’t Matter**: Google’s systems don’t track freelancers’ employment history. They only care about the content **on your site right now**.
*   **Example**: A chef who once worked at a bad restaurant can still cook amazing meals at a new place.

#### **Key Takeaway**

Hiring freelancers is **not risky**—as long as they create high-quality content for _your_ site. Google rewards helpful content, no matter who writes it.

**Danny Sullivan’s Advice**:

> _**“Freelancers aren’t the issue. Focus on making your content the best it can be.”**_

* * *

**TL;DR**: Freelancers are fine! Just make sure their work is original, useful, and tailored to your audience. Google’s bots judge content, not resumes. 😊
