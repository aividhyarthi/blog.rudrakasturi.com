An advertisement can be rejected even when its headline looks harmless. The image may contain a prohibited claim. The landing page may promote a restricted product. The business may not meet advertiser eligibility requirements. The ad may also be inconsistent with the product or offer it claims to represent.

OpenAI has added more context to rejected-ad messages in ChatGPT Ads Manager. Advertisers can now see more information about the policy involved and decide whether to revise the advertisement or request a review. The Policy Center can be opened from the dropdown in the bottom-left corner of Ads Manager.

OpenAI says the approval guidelines have not changed. The update makes review feedback more useful; it does not create a new set of rules.

## How ChatGPT Ads review works

OpenAI's published Ads Policies describe review at three levels.

### 1. Advertiser review

OpenAI assesses whether an advertiser is a legitimate business and can safely participate in the advertising ecosystem. This may include advertiser verification, account quality checks and signals related to scams, fraud, abuse or deceptive behaviour.

### 2. Creative and landing-page review

The review covers the ad title, description, image or video and destination page. A compliant headline does not make a non-compliant landing page acceptable.

### 3. Placement review

Approved ads are intended to appear only in relevant conversations that meet OpenAI's placement and safety requirements. Approval does not guarantee that an ad will appear beside every conversation or topic.

OpenAI says automated systems, including machine-learning models and classifiers, are used in review, with human oversight and escalation in some cases.

## Common reasons an ad may be rejected

The following are practical categories based on OpenAI's published policy. They are not a claim that every rejection uses one of these exact labels.

### Misleading or deceptive claims

Claims about price, performance, results, endorsements, affiliations or comparisons must be accurate and supportable.

Avoid:
- Guaranteed results without evidence.
- Fake testimonials or endorsements.
- A discount that is not available on the landing page.
- Claims that imply an official OpenAI partnership when none exists.
- A headline that promises one product but links to another.

### Restricted or disallowed categories

OpenAI's policies list categories that are disallowed or restricted during the current rollout. Examples include political advertising, gambling, alcohol and drugs, weapons, adult content and other sensitive or regulated categories. Some financial, healthcare and legal advertising may be considered only under limited approval arrangements and additional safeguards.

Do not assume that a category is eligible because the same product can be advertised on Google or Meta. Check the current OpenAI policy and market eligibility.

### Inconsistent landing pages

The creative and destination must describe the same offer. An ad for a permitted product should not lead to a page that prominently promotes a prohibited or unrelated product.

### Offensive or shocking material

OpenAI's baseline standards prohibit obscene, vulgar or shocking language and imagery. Review the image itself, including text embedded inside it.

### Privacy, safety and sensitive contexts

OpenAI's placement rules are designed to prevent ads appearing in sensitive or vulnerable user contexts. Advertisers should not design campaigns around exploiting personal distress, health concerns or other sensitive situations.

### Inaccessible or unreviewable destinations

The landing page must be valid and reachable. OpenAI's ad creation guidance says advertisers should not block the relevant OpenAI ad and search crawlers. A page that cannot be reviewed may not be eligible.

## What to do when an ad is rejected

1. Open Ads Manager and locate the rejected ad.
2. Read the rejection message and identify the policy area mentioned.
3. Open the Policy Center from the dropdown at the bottom-left of Ads Manager.
4. Compare the policy with the headline, description, image or video, advertiser details and landing page.
5. Identify the exact asset or claim that needs attention.
6. Revise the smallest necessary part of the ad or destination page.
7. Submit the corrected version for review.
8. If you believe the decision is incorrect, use the available review or appeal route in the account rather than repeatedly resubmitting the same unchanged ad.

Keep a record of the original rejection, the policy cited, the change made and the review outcome. This helps teams identify recurring creative or landing-page problems.

## ChatGPT Ads approval checklist: do this

- Describe the product or service accurately.
- Ensure prices, discounts and availability match the landing page.
- Use images that genuinely represent the offer.
- Make the advertiser identity clear.
- Check the entire destination page, not only the page above the fold.
- Confirm that the product category is permitted in the target market.
- Keep claims factual and supportable.
- Ensure the landing page loads and can be reviewed.
- Use clear, professional language.
- Review current policies before launching a new category or campaign.

## Do not do this

- Do not invent customer testimonials, awards or endorsements.
- Do not imply that OpenAI endorses your product.
- Do not make guaranteed health, financial or business outcome claims.
- Do not use a permitted ad to route people to a disallowed product.
- Do not hide important conditions, fees or limitations.
- Do not use shocking imagery merely to attract attention.
- Do not use misleading countdowns or false scarcity.
- Do not repeatedly submit the same rejected asset without addressing the reason.
- Do not assume approval on another advertising platform means approval on ChatGPT.
- Do not treat a rejection as proof that the entire business is permanently banned; read the account-specific message.

## A practical rejection diagnosis table

| Rejection area | What to inspect | Possible correction |
|---|---|---|
| Advertiser eligibility | Business identity and verification | Complete required business information |
| Creative claim | Headline, copy and embedded image text | Remove unsupported or misleading claims |
| Category restriction | Product and service being promoted | Check eligibility and any pre-approval route |
| Landing page mismatch | Destination content, price and product | Link to a page that matches the ad |
| Image or video | Visual content and implied claims | Replace non-compliant or confusing creative |
| Technical access | Page availability and crawler access | Fix errors or access restrictions |
| Placement safety | Sensitive or vulnerable contexts | Follow placement rules; do not target sensitive conversations |

## Does a rejection mean the policy has changed?

No. The November 25 update is described as more detailed rejection explanations. OpenAI says the approval guidelines themselves have not changed.

That distinction is important. A more detailed explanation helps advertisers diagnose a problem. It does not mean previously disallowed products or claims have become acceptable.

## The larger lesson for advertisers

ChatGPT Ads review is not just a copy check. It is an assessment of the advertiser, the full creative and the destination experience.

The safest approach is to treat the ad and landing page as one connected promise. If the headline says one thing, the image suggests another and the destination sells something different, the campaign creates both a policy risk and a poor customer experience.

**Before asking why an ad was rejected, check what the complete customer journey is communicating.**
