**Quick answer:** To show up when someone types a word into Instagram's search bar, make that word appear in places Search is reported to read: your username, your bio, your captions and your on-screen text, consistently, across multiple posts on the same topic. This is distinct from ranking well in Feed or Explore, which reportedly weigh engagement and personalization more heavily.

**Best for:** brands and creators trying to be found when someone searches a topic or product category inside Instagram, not just trying to go viral.

## Search is not the same system as Feed

Marketing guides describe Instagram as running several separate AI-powered ranking systems, one each for Feed, Reels, Stories, Explore and Search, rather than one algorithm deciding everything. Search is repeatedly described as the most keyword-driven of these, closer to a small search engine than to a recommendation feed. That distinction matters: tactics that drive Reels reach (trending audio, fast cuts, a strong first-second hook) are not the same tactics that get you found by someone typing a specific word.

## What Search reportedly reads

Guides consistently list these fields as inputs to Instagram Search:

- **Username and bio** — reportedly weighted highly, since they are permanent and topic-stable, unlike individual captions.
- **Captions** — the specific words you use matter, not just the general topic.
- **Hashtags** — described by one source as attributed to Adam Mosseri as helping search specifically, while not increasing reach on their own. If accurate, that means hashtags are a search-discovery tool, not a reach lever, and should be chosen for how someone would actually search, not for volume.
- **On-screen text and spoken audio in Reels** — reportedly read for Search the same way they are read for Reels ranking generally.

## What to do

1. **Write your bio and username with the exact words a customer would search**, not just your brand name if the brand name alone isn't descriptive. "Jaipur Wedding Photography — [Brand]" tells Search more than the brand name alone.
2. **Use the same core keyword across multiple posts over time.** A single well-optimized post is weaker signal than ten posts that consistently use the same handful of topic words in captions and on-screen text.
3. **Choose hashtags for search intent, not volume.** If hashtags help search but not reach, as reported, pick the ones a real searcher would type, even if they have lower post counts than a generic massive hashtag.
4. **Add on-screen text to Reels and carousels that states the topic plainly**, especially if audio alone carries your message.
5. **Avoid diluting an account's topic.** An account that posts about five unrelated subjects gives Search mixed signals about what it should be found for.
6. **Test it the honest way.** Log out, or ask someone who doesn't follow you, to search your target terms inside Instagram and tell you what comes up. Your own search results are personalized by your activity and will not show you what a stranger sees.

## How this differs from ranking in Google

Instagram Search only affects what people find inside the app. If you also want Reels and posts to surface in Google AI Mode or AI Overviews, that depends on separate factors: whether the account allows search engine indexing, and whether Google's systems choose to cite it. See [how to rank Instagram Reels in Google AI Mode and AI Overviews](/how-to-rank-your-instagram-reels-in-google-ai-mode-and-ai-overviews/) for that side of it. Optimizing for both at once is possible, since clear, keyword-honest captions and on-screen text help with each, but they are not the same ranking system and should not be assumed to move together.
