# Seeing a click drop in Generative AI in GSC since Aug 12? It's a logging bug, not a ranking loss.

A number of site owners started flagging a strange dip in Google Search Console clicks this week. Visibility tools show nothing wrong, and analytics shows nothing wrong. Google has now confirmed it's a reporting glitch, not a real drop in traffic.

4 MIN READ·SEARCH CONSOLE·SEO OPS·AS OF AUG 18, 2026

If your Search Console Performance report has looked off since around August 12, you're not imagining it, and you're not alone. SEOs and site owners started comparing notes this week: a visible dip in reported clicks in GSC, starting on that date, with nothing else lining up to explain it.

The tell is what isn't moving. Rank tracking and visibility tools show no change. GA4 and other analytics platforms show no drop in organic sessions. The only thing behaving strangely is the clicks number inside Search Console itself, which is exactly the pattern you'd expect from a counting problem rather than an actual loss of traffic.

## The exchange

Here's roughly how it played out publicly: someone in the SEO community flagged the anomaly, and Google's Search Liaison account responded within the same news cycle, acknowledging the issue and attributing it to logging rather than anything happening in Search itself.

SEO / SITE OWNER

Flagged a strange dip in clicks starting around Aug 12 in Search Console, possibly a bug, since visibility reporting looked stable elsewhere and analytics showed no corresponding drop.

GOOGLE SEARCH LIAISON CONFIRMED: LOGGING ISSUE

Confirmed awareness of the issue and said it's being worked on. Said it's purely a logging problem and does not reflect any actual change in Search visibility, and that an annotation would be added to Search Console to flag it publicly.

## How to tell if this is you

AUG 12 GSC CLICKS ↓ ANALYTICS → VISIBILITY →

ONE LINE MOVES. TWO DON'T. THAT'S THE SIGNATURE OF A LOGGING BUG, NOT A TRAFFIC LOSS.

If GSC clicks fell starting around August 12 but your rank tracker, your visibility or share-of-voice reporting, and your GA4 organic sessions all look normal for the same window, this is very likely the same reporting issue. If your rankings actually dropped too, or your analytics sessions actually fell, that's a different, real problem and worth investigating on its own terms, not writing off as a glitch.

## What to do about it right now

01

**Don't act on it yet**Resist the urge to overhaul content, bidding, or crawl budget in response to a GSC-only click dip. There's nothing to fix on your end if this is the cause.

02

**Cross-check against two other sources**Pull GA4 organic sessions and, if you track it, a third-party visibility or rank tool for the same date range. Agreement across those two is your confirmation.

03

**Get ahead of client questions**If you manage accounts for clients, send a short heads-up now rather than waiting for them to ask why "traffic dropped." A pre-emptive note reads as competence, not panic.

04

**Watch for the official annotation**Google said it will add a note directly inside Search Console flagging the affected window. Once that lands, it becomes your definitive reference point for reporting.

05

**Hold off on YoY and MoM click comparisons touching this window**Any report that spans the affected dates will look artificially worse than reality until Google clarifies exactly which days and metrics were hit.

## Why this keeps happening

Search Console has had a rough 2026 on the data-integrity front, this is not the first logging anomaly this year, and it likely won't be the last. None of that makes any single incident less disruptive when it lands in your reporting week, but it's worth building the habit now: treat a GSC-only anomaly as a hypothesis to confirm, not a fact to report to a client, until at least one independent source agrees with it.

**Where this stands as of Aug 18, 2026:** Google has acknowledged the issue and attributed it to logging, not a real change in Search visibility, and says an annotation is coming to Search Console. No fix timeline or exact affected-metric breakdown has been published yet. This post will read differently once that lands, treat it as the situation as currently understood, not a final resolution.
