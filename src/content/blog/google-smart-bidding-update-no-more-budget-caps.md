Google Ads · Rolling out Aug 17, 2026

# The Budget Governor Is Being Switched Off

For years, a budget cap quietly overrode your Target CPA or Target ROAS whenever a campaign was performing too well to spend its full budget. On **Aug 17**, Google is removing that override. From that date, the target is the target — budget or no budget.

## What Google actually changed

Ahead of the rollout, Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin walked through the mechanics advertisers have been asking about. The short version: **bid targets become the primary lever** for campaign efficiency, even when a campaign is budget-constrained. Campaigns that have been quietly beating their Target CPA or Target ROAS — because the budget cap never let Smart Bidding push all the way to the target — will now be optimized toward the actual number you set, not past it.

The stated reason is **predictability**. Right now, raising or lowering a budget can suddenly let Smart Bidding "catch up" toward the stated target, producing a jolt in CPA or ROAS that looks like a mystery unless you know this mechanic exists. After Aug 17, the target holds steady through those budget changes, so the swings should be smaller and easier to explain to a client or a CFO.

### Before Aug 17

*   Budget cap can silently override your target, letting real CPA/ROAS run better than what you set
*   Increasing budget triggers a re-adjustment back toward target — a sudden, unexplained dip in efficiency
*   Your "target" is aspirational, not a promise, in budget-limited campaigns

### After Aug 17

*   Target CPA/ROAS is respected as the real ceiling, regardless of budget headroom
*   Budget changes shouldn't cause the same performance whiplash
*   Google adds in-account notifications + a review tool to flag campaigns with a target/actual gap

**Unaffected:** campaigns without budget constraints see no change — Target CPA/ROAS already fully governs spend and efficiency there. This only touches accounts where budget has been the invisible hand.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

Most advertisers set targets once, early, and forget them — because the budget cap was quietly forgiving loose targets for months or years. That forgiveness ends on Aug 17. If your real performance has been running meaningfully better than your stated target, Smart Bidding won't reward that gap anymore; it'll optimize down toward the number you actually typed in, which can mean **less volume** even though efficiency "improves" on paper.

For India accounts specifically — where budget caps are routinely used as the primary control (agencies managing many small-budget SMB or D2C accounts, festive-season spend spikes, marketplace sellers on tight ROAS) — this is the kind of change that shows up as a volume or lead-flow drop with no obvious cause, unless someone audits targets first.

## What to do before Aug 17

1.  01**Pull the gap report.** For every budget-limited campaign, compare stated Target CPA/ROAS against actual CPA/ROAS over the last 30–60 days.
2.  02**Flag the outperformers.** Any campaign where actual is comfortably better than target is the one that will lose volume first — that gap is about to close.
3.  03**Decide: loosen the target, or accept less volume.** If the gap reflects genuine headroom, widen the target now rather than getting surprised by a spend drop on the 18th.
4.  04**Use Google's own review tool.** The in-account notification flagging target/actual gaps is the fastest way to triage a large account without pulling every campaign manually.
5.  05**Brief stakeholders now.** If a client or founder is watching a lead-flow dashboard, tell them a platform-level bidding change is coming — before the numbers move, not after.

The bottom line: after Aug 17, the number in your Target CPA/ROAS field stops being a suggestion and starts being the actual ceiling on your campaign's efficiency — budget or no budget. Anyone who's been letting a generous budget do the work of a tight target should expect the platform to catch up to what they typed in, not what they've been getting away with.
