Today, Google announced a new change to how it ranks search results, called the November 2024 core update. This update aims to show people more helpful and reliable content when they search online and to reduce the presence of content that feels like it was created just to rank highly in search results.

### Why This Update?

Google wants to improve the search experience by focusing on content that truly helps people. This update is a way of telling website creators, “Make content that’s useful for people, not just for search engines.” If a site offers valuable information, it’s more likely to show up higher in search results. On the other hand, if a website is just stuffed with keywords or overly optimized tricks, it may not rank as well after this update.

### How This Affects Websites

For anyone who owns a website or creates content, this update is a reminder: focus on making content that your audience finds helpful and engaging. Sites that prioritize genuine content might see better rankings, while those that are “too optimized” might notice a drop. This shift encourages more natural, authentic information, which benefits everyone.

### When and Where to Follow This Update

The rollout of this update will take up to two weeks. During this time, you might notice some ups and downs in website rankings. Google has also added this update to its [Search status dashboard](https://lnkd.in/ezpkZUJw) where you can follow the rollout progress. For a deeper look at how these updates work, check out Google’s guide on core updates [here](https://lnkd.in/gjkpgQxy).

### What This Means for Search Users

For anyone using Google, this update means search results should feel more relevant and trustworthy. In short, Google’s goal is to make it easier for people to find helpful content and avoid results that feel like they were just made to attract clicks.

Google’s goal is simple: show people content that genuinely answers their questions or meets their needs. ### Tips for Website Owners

If you notice a drop in your site’s position in search results, here are some steps to help you understand and adjust:

1.  **Check for Traffic Drops in Search Console**  
    If your site’s traffic or ranking position drops around the time of a core update, use **Google Search Console** to investigate. It can help you determine if you need to make any changes.
2.  **Wait for the Update to Complete**  
    The update will take up to two weeks to fully roll out. Keep an eye on Google’s [Search Status Dashboard](https://lnkd.in/ezpkZUJw) to confirm when it’s complete. To get the most accurate analysis, wait at least one full week after the update is done before comparing data.
3.  **Compare Dates in Search Console**  
    After a week has passed, try comparing site performance from the week after the update completed to the week before it started. This helps you pinpoint any specific changes.
4.  **Review Your Top Pages and Queries**  
    Look at your highest-ranking pages and queries. Did they have a minor or significant drop in position?
    *   **Small Drop** (e.g., from position 2 to 4): Often, no major changes are necessary; avoid altering content that’s already performing well.
    *   **Large Drop** (e.g., from position 4 to 29): This might require a closer look and a deeper assessment of content quality and relevance.
5.  **Analyze Different Search Types Separately**  
    Check different search types in Search Console (e.g., Web Search, Google Images, Video, or News) to understand where the drop occurred. This can give insight into where improvements may be needed.
