Every client call I'm on right now has the same slide. "Our AI Share of Voice is 23%." Or "We're mentioned in 1 out of 3 ChatGPT answers." Everyone's excited. Nobody's asked the next question: is this number even real?

I've been running AI visibility audits for clients across several industries for the past few months. And I've started to notice something. Most of the metrics being reported aren't wrong exactly. They're just not telling you what people think they're telling you.

Here's the noise. And here's what I think you should actually look at instead.

* * *

## The market is obsessed with one number: Share of Voice

Walk into any AI visibility tool — HubSpot AEO, AirOps, Profound, half a dozen others — and the first number on the dashboard is some version of Share of Voice. How often does your brand get mentioned, compared to competitors, across a set of prompts run through ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, whatever.

It sounds like market share. It gets treated like market share. Teams report it to leadership the same way they'd report search rankings.

There's a problem with that. One industry report found the average brand mention rate across AI answers sits at just 17.2%. That single stat gets used to build entire strategies. But almost nobody checks how stable that number actually is from one day to the next.

* * *

## Why the number you're reporting is probably noise

This is the part that doesn't get talked about enough.

AI citation data doesn't behave like search rankings. In Google, if you rank #3 for a keyword, you mostly stay around #3 for weeks. The system is stable.

AI answers don't work that way. Citation share follows a pattern where a few sources get cited a lot and most get cited rarely — and that overall pattern holds steady. But which sources land in the "rarely cited" bucket can shuffle wildly from one test run to the next.

One detailed study backs this up with hard numbers. It used statistical resampling and found that many of the visibility differences brands report between themselves and competitors actually fall inside the margin of error. In plain terms: the "difference" isn't a real difference at all. It's just noise.

So if your dashboard says "we're at 23%, up from 19% last month" — that 4-point move might be real. Or it might be nothing. The honest practice is to treat every citation-share number as a range, not a single figure, and only call it a trend if this month's range doesn't overlap with last month's.

Almost no tool reports it this way. Almost every team presents it as a clean, stable number anyway.

## The second problem: mention rate isn't the same as influence

Here's something I had to learn the hard way while building visibility reports for a client. Two brands can both "appear" in an AI answer. One actually shapes what the AI says. The other is a footnote nobody reads.

One 2026 framework studying over 21,000 citations draws a useful line between two things: getting listed as a source, versus actually shaping the answer the AI gives. Perplexity tends to cite a lot of sources per answer, but leans lightly on each one. ChatGPT cites fewer sources, but pulls more from each one it does use.

What that means in practice: you can show up in Perplexity's source list and have zero actual influence on what it told the user. Or you can get cited once by ChatGPT and have it shape two full paragraphs of the answer.

A flat "we were mentioned" number hides this completely. The smarter approach separates "appears anywhere in the answer" from "actually cited as a source, and positioned as the recommendation." High mentions with low real positioning usually means you're in the conversation but not the recommendation.

* * *

## The third problem: one engine is not the market

This is the one I'd flag hardest to anyone reading their AI visibility dashboard right now.

Run the same brand through the same set of prompts, and the share of voice you get can be wildly different depending on which AI engine you check. One documented case showed a brand at 10.1% visibility on Perplexity and just 0.2% on Gemini — for the exact same questions.

That's not a small gap. That's the difference between "we have a real presence" and "we basically don't exist," for the same brand, same questions, same week.

Same brand. Same prompts. Four very different answers. Why does this happen? Each AI engine pulls from different places. ChatGPT leans heavily on earned media and elite news sources. Perplexity pulls heavily from Reddit. Claude prefers long-form editorial writing. Google's AI Overviews lean heavily on YouTube.

So if your content strategy is built around getting picked up by traditional press, you might be winning big on ChatGPT and invisible on Perplexity. A single blended "AI visibility score" averages all of this away and tells you nothing useful about where to actually put your effort.

* * *

## And citations don't last

One more thing that doesn't show up on most dashboards: how fast this all changes.

Research tracking AI citation behavior found that a brand showing up in a ChatGPT answer today has roughly a 40 to 60% chance of being swapped out for a different source within a month. Not because the brand did anything wrong. Simply because the AI refreshed what it's pulling from.

The same research found 70% of all AI-cited domains churn out within six months. That's a brutal number if you're used to SEO, where a page that ranks well tends to keep ranking well for a long stretch.

This is also why a single audit, done once, is close to useless. A tool reporting "you appear in 40% of AI answers" off one snapshot might be reporting a number that would show up as 25% or 55% on a different day. Without repeated testing, the number is close to meaningless.

* * *

## So what should you actually track?

Here's where this gets simple, and I want to be direct about it, because the easy misreading of everything above is "stop tracking Share of Voice and mentions, track something else instead." That's not the point.

There are only 2 metrics that matter. Everything else you've heard — Share of Voice, visibility score, citation share — is just a different name for one of these 2, or a way of slicing one of them.

**1\. Mention rate.** Does your brand name show up anywhere in the AI's answer — even just as text, no link, no source credit. This is the weakest signal. Easy to get, easy to feel good about, doesn't tell you much.

**2\. Citation rate.** Is your actual domain or page named or linked as a source the AI pulled from. This is the one that matters. It's closer to "the AI trusts this page enough to point to it," and it's the one with any real link to traffic. **"Share of Voice" is just citation rate, measured against your competitors** — same metric, just framed competitively. It's not a third thing.

One more thing worth ruling out clearly: there's no "ranking" in AI answers, not the way there is in Google Search. A search results page has positions 1 through 10. An AI answer doesn't — for any given prompt, you're either named as a source or you're not. So don't go looking for a "rank" number here. There isn't one.

**You're already tracking the right 2 numbers, mention rate and citation rate. The problem was never the metric. It's how it's being measured.** So nothing below is a new metric to add to your dashboard. It's 3 fixes to how you measure the 2 numbers you already have.

Right now, you probably measure it like this…

…fix it by measuring it like this

One test run, reported as a single number ("23%")

Same prompts, run several times, reported as a range ("18–26%")

All engines averaged into one score

ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews — reported separately

Brand name shows up anywhere = counted as a win

Only counted as a win if your domain is actually named as a source

That's the whole fix. Same 2 metrics. Measured properly instead of measured lazily.

One layer underneath citation rate worth tracking too: **which specific pages** are getting cited, not just whether your domain as a whole is. Citation rate tells you if you're showing up at all. The page-level breakdown tells you where — which of your pages the AI trusts enough to pull from, and which ones never get touched. That's the difference between "we have AI visibility" and "we know exactly which 12 pages are earning it."

Two more things worth knowing once you're measuring it this way — not new metrics, just context for reading the 2 numbers correctly:

**Don't trust a one-time report.** Citations churn fast. A number from a single audit, done once, can be 20 points off from what you'd see next week. Re-run the same prompt set every few weeks so you're watching a trend, not a screenshot.

**Know where your citations actually come from.** One large study of 75,000 brands found 82% of all AI citations trace back to earned media — coverage from other publications, not a brand's own website. If your plan is "publish more on our own blog" and your category's citations mostly come from outside coverage, that plan won't move your number much.

* * *

## The bottom line

None of this means AI visibility doesn't matter. It clearly does — buyers are increasingly asking AI tools for recommendations before they ever open a search engine. What it means is that most of what's being reported as "AI visibility" right now is a single, noisy snapshot dressed up as a stable fact.

If a number can flip by 20 points depending on which day you ran it, it's not a metric. It's a guess with a percentage sign on it.

Track ranges, not single snapshots. Track engines separately, not blended together. And only count a citation as a win if you're actually named as a source, not just mentioned somewhere in the text. Same 2 numbers as before — just measured honestly instead of measured lazily. That's the difference between a dashboard that looks good in a meeting, and one that actually tells you something true.
