The News/Media Alliance (NMA) has expressed strong opposition to Google’s recent changes to its site reputation abuse policy, urging federal agencies to intervene. In a letter addressed to the Department of Justice and the Federal Trade Commission, the NMA criticized the policy changes, stating they jeopardize the visibility and viability of legitimate news outlets that rely on affiliate links and partner content for revenue and traffic.

### Threat to Media Revenue Streams

According to the NMA, Google’s policy revision could lead to reliable media properties being removed from search results for publishing affiliate or partner-driven content. The alliance claims this decision could eliminate critical revenue sources for already struggling news organizations, further amplifying the challenges posed by Google’s so-called “walled garden” strategy, which keeps users within its ecosystem and limits external traffic.

“This move treats important media partnerships as exploitative marketing practices,” the NMA said. “It undermines the financial health of news companies in a way disconnected from the actual value or protection offered to Google users.”

The NMA warned that this decision has the potential to decimate affiliate content traffic and revenue streams, which are lifelines for many news organizations.

### Google’s Perspective

In its blog post announcing the update, Google explained that the policy aims to combat abusive use of third-party content. The tech giant stated it considers various factors to determine if such content is being misused, adding that it doesn’t solely rely on a site’s claims about content production.

The NMA, however, views this stance as overreach. “This is yet another example of Google placing itself in between news publishers and their readers,” the letter stated.

### My Perspective

This policy change raises fundamental questions about the power imbalance between tech platforms and content creators. As someone deeply involved in the digital media landscape, I see this as yet another move by Google to exert control over the ecosystem, prioritizing its own interests over those of publishers.

While combating abuse and ensuring user protection are valid goals, applying broad-brush policies to legitimate news organizations undermines trust and innovation in the media industry. Affiliate and partner content are not just revenue streams—they’re tools that help publishers diversify income in an era where advertising revenues are dominated by the likes of Google and Meta.

The danger lies in Google’s unilateral decision-making. With its vast influence over search and traffic, the company holds an outsized role in determining the financial viability of news outlets. These actions risk stifling smaller or independent media organizations that rely on affiliate partnerships to compete.

Moreover, this highlights a critical need for collaboration, not unilateral action. News organizations must be included in policy discussions, ensuring that any changes address abuse without collateral damage to legitimate actors. A lack of transparency and consultation only widens the gap between platforms and publishers, leading to mistrust and friction.

### Calls for Intervention

The letter, signed by Danielle Coffey, president and CEO of the News/Media Alliance, highlights the pressing need for regulatory scrutiny. It emphasizes the detrimental impact of Google’s policies on the already fragile financial state of the news industry and questions the fairness of applying such measures to trusted media organizations.

### Industry Implications

The NMA’s plea underscores a growing tension between major tech platforms and the news industry. As digital advertising revenues increasingly flow to platforms like Google, news organizations have become heavily reliant on affiliate links and content partnerships to stay afloat. The policy change adds yet another layer of uncertainty to an industry grappling with financial sustainability in the digital age.

By calling for federal intervention, the NMA hopes to not only protect revenue streams for news organizations but also advocate for a more equitable digital ecosystem that doesn’t disproportionately favor tech giants over the media industry.
