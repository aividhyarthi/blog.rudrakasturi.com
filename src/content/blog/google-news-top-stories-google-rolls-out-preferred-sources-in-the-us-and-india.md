**Date:** August 12, 2025

Google has launched its [**Preferred Sources**](https://blog.google/products/search/preferred-sources/) feature for all users in the United States and India, expanding beyond its initial testing in Search Labs. The feature is currently available for English-language searches.

![Screenshot of Google Search interface displaying the 'Top Stories' section, with options to choose preferred news sources for personalized results.](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/image-67-1024x512.webp)

Preferred Sources lets users choose specific news outlets, blogs, or websites they want to see more often in Google Search’s **Top Stories** section.

To set it up, users can search for a topic, click the star icon next to the "Top Stories" header, and add their preferred sites. Once saved, stories from these sources will appear more prominently in results, either directly in Top Stories or in a new **“From your sources”** section.

Users who tested the feature earlier in Search Labs will have their preferences carried over automatically, with the option to update them at any time.

Google says the feature is designed to give people more control over the sources they see in news search results while helping trusted publishers strengthen audience connections.

**Google says: “people really value being able to select a range of sources - with over half of users choosing four or more.”**

### How It Works (Your Quick Walkthrough)

1.  **Search a current topic or event.**
2.  **Click the star icon** located beside the "Top Stories" header.
3.  **Select and add your preferred sources**—you can choose as many as you like (many users opt for four or more)
4.  **Refresh the search results.** Your chosen sources will now appear more frequently in Top Stories or in a dedicated **“From your sources”** section
5.  **Already a Labs user?** Your existing preferences are automatically carried over—though you can always update them anytime

### Why This Matters

*   **For Users**: This empowers searchers with greater control over the news they see, aligning with personal preferences rather than solely algorithmic choices.
*   **For Publishers**: Appearing as a preferred source can significantly increase visibility and drive organic traffic—especially as Top Stories remains a powerful discovery channel
