Who decided that politics must always sit on the front page?  
Was it written in the Constitution?  
Was there a law passed by Parliament?  
Or did we all just assume that’s how it’s supposed to be and kept repeating it for a hundred years?

That’s the problem with legacy.  
No one remembers why it started.  
They just keep polishing it.

## **The Tradition We Never Questioned**

For decades, every Indian newspaper has looked the same: politics and government on the front, entertainment at the back.

Editors will tell you it’s about ethics and seriousness. They’ll say the front page is “sacred space.”

But if you ask readers, they’ll tell you the truth - it’s the least read page in the paper.

The front page isn’t where people go to _feel_. It’s where they go to _confirm what they already know._

## **The Digital Reality Check**

Now open your phone.  
Scroll YouTube, Instagram, or Spotify.  
Every home screen is designed around **what people love**, not **what editors respect.**

Music. Fashion. OTT. Bigg Boss clips.  
That’s the new “front page” of India.

Look at the data:

*   OTT platforms in India have over **45 million paying subscribers.**
*   Premium news apps together barely cross **2 million.**
*   On a Sunday, **Bigg Boss live streams pull 12–13 lakh concurrent viewers.**

That’s the real editorial priority of India - **timepass over tension.**

The audience already voted.  
We just never counted their ballots.

## **The Economics No One Talks About**

If you’ve worked in a newsroom, you know this:  
news doesn’t make money.  
Entertainment does.

Politics brings prestige.  
Bollywood brings subscriptions.

Sports fills the back page,  
but it also fills the ad slots.

And yet, the front page that most expensive real estate in print is still given to the same tired headlines:  
Cabinet reshuffles, speeches, allegations, denials.

None of which sell copies or build habit.  
But they make the editor feel “serious.”

Legacy media has confused **dignity** with **relevance.**

## **The Power Play Behind It**

Let’s be blunt - the government loves that the front page still worships politics. It keeps attention on power, not people.

In colonial times, British papers printed their crimes on page one to intimidate Indians. After independence, Indian editors did the same not to scare citizens, but to impress them.

So the cycle continued.  
Politics on top.  
People at the bottom.

And 75 years later, the headlines changed,  
but the habit didn’t.

## **The New Front Page Is Already Here**

Digital media broke that rule and never looked back.  
They let the audience decide what leads.

Open YouTube: the lead story is what’s trending.  
Open Instagram: the headline is a reel.  
Open Spotify: the top news is music.

Algorithms have done what legacy editors refused to **democratize curiosity.**

And the irony?  
The same editors who mock “entertainment culture” are now producing podcasts and shorts to stay relevant.

Because that’s where the readers and the revenue went.

## **The Truth Legacy Editors Won’t Admit**

Most newsrooms today quietly depend on lifestyle, spirituality, or celebrity verticals for survival.  
A newspaper’s entertainment coverage is its oxygen, its politics coverage is its posture.

Ask anyone in a media house - 80% of their traffic, ads, and brand deals come from **non-news** content.

And yet, every morning, they still print a front page that pretends otherwise.

That’s not journalism.  
That’s denial in print.

## **So, Who Said Politics Belongs on Page One?**

Maybe the British did.  
Maybe the freedom fighters continued it.  
Maybe the editors defended it.  
But no one asked if the audience ever agreed.

In 2025, a podcaster’s interview is more valuable than a prime-time bulletin.  
A YouTuber breaking down budget memes will reach more people than a newspaper editorial.  
That’s not because people are shallow - it’s because **the medium finally reflects their mood.**

The front page was built to control the citizen. The feed is built to connect the human.

## **The Real Question**

So, should newspapers adapt or die as martyrs of tradition?

Maybe it’s not about giving up ethics. It’s about giving up legacy.

If the goal of journalism is to _inform the public_, then meet them where they already are - not where your legacy says they should be.

If the readers’ home screens are filled with entertainment, spirituality, and wellness, then maybe those are the stories that reflect the _real India_, not the _official one._

## **My Thought**s

Respect to every editor who still believes the front page must stand tall. But relevance isn’t about standing tall anymore - it’s about showing up where people are looking.

The news no longer sits on the front page. It sits between two reels, one podcast, and a short attention span.

The world didn’t kill the newspaper. The newspaper killed its curiosity.

So think again  
who said politics deserves the front page, when the people already turned the page?
