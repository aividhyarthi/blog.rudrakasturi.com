On **October 25, 2024**, Google hosted a **Search Fundamentals Training** at their Gurugram office for Indian news publishers. The training covered critical areas for improving website performance in Google Search, including how news content appears on Search, best practices for news websites, and updates on Google's algorithms.

### Best Practices for News Websites

One of the top recommendations was to focus on **internal linking**. Creating a strong internal link structure helps Google prioritize important content, especially during major events. This involves linking smaller related stories back to the main story, which signals to Google that the main story holds higher importance.

Another important update was that **meta keywords** are no longer relevant. Google has stopped recognizing meta keywords, so adding them serves no purpose in terms of ranking.

### Avoid Keyword Stuffing and Content Duplication

The training stressed avoiding **keyword repetition**. Google’s algorithm has become smarter and no longer rewards excessive keyword use. Overuse can actually harm your rankings. Instead, publishers should focus on writing naturally for readers.

It was also clarified that **content duplication within a site doesn’t hurt rankings**, but duplicating content across different sites can lead to penalties for plagiarism.

### Write for Users First

Google’s evolving algorithm prioritizes **user experience** and engagement. Instead of writing for SEO alone, publishers were encouraged to focus on providing meaningful content for their readers. User satisfaction will naturally lead to better rankings.

### AI Overviews: Potential for Error

Google’s **AI Overviews**, while based on reputable sources, can sometimes surface inaccurate or "hallucinated" information. It’s important for publishers to keep this in mind, especially when relying on AI-driven search summaries for news content.

### Core Updates: No Penalties, But Content Matters

Google clarified that **core updates** are not intended to penalize websites. Instead, these updates are designed to surface higher-quality content. If your content adheres to best practices and provides real value, your site is less likely to experience a traffic dip during a core update.

### Discover Traffic: Volatility Alert

Google emphasized that **Google Discover traffic** is highly volatile, and publishers should not rely on it as their primary traffic source. Instead, the focus should be on building **sustained traffic** through authoritative content.

### URL Structure and Keywords

A significant takeaway about **URLs** is that **keywords in URLs have minimal impact on rankings**. While it’s beneficial to have clean, meaningful URLs for users, they do not significantly influence search rankings. Content quality, rather than the URL structure, should remain the primary focus for SEO.

### Syndicated Content Should Be "Noindex"

Publishers using syndicated content (such as news from agencies) should ensure that it’s marked as **"noindex"** to avoid being flagged for duplicate content by Google. Alternatively, they should consider rewriting syndicated content to make it unique.

### Use of Wikipedia to Identify Topics

Though not explicitly stated, it was inferred that **Wikipedia** is a useful tool for identifying trending topics and building authority. Google closely monitors Wikipedia for identifying entities and establishing credibility on various subjects.

### Auto Translation and Hreflang Tags

Google auto-translates search results based on the user’s location, which can sometimes cause issues with ranking. For instance, a website in a different language may rank higher than one written in the query’s original language. To avoid this, publishers should use **hreflang meta tags** to properly signal content in different languages and avoid competing with their own multilingual versions.

### Core Web Vitals and AMP

Although **Core Web Vitals** are important for user experience, Google emphasized that **content quality** still plays a larger role in rankings. Additionally, **AMP (Accelerated Mobile Pages)** is no longer a required format, and any fast-loading mobile page has equal chances of ranking.

### Backlinks and AI-Generated Content

Google stated that **backlinks** from external websites are still valuable but should not be the sole focus. Quality content that naturally attracts links is key. Additionally, using **AI to generate content** is acceptable as long as the content is high-quality and is manually reviewed before publishing.

### More Insights and Resources

*   **Handling Paywalls**: Use paywall markup to allow Google to index the article while restricting user access behind a paywall.
*   **Images and Videos**: While they don’t directly affect rankings, using images and videos can help content appear in additional search areas, such as **Image Search** or **Video Search**.

### Final Thoughts

The **Google Search Fundamentals Training** provided valuable insights for news publishers to optimize their content for search engines. Publishers should focus on **user-first content**, avoid keyword stuffing, manage syndicated content properly, and build authority in topics through internal linking and unique content. Understanding the volatility of **Discover traffic** and the nuances of **core updates** will also help publishers maintain strong search performance over time.

By adhering to these best practices, publishers can ensure their sites stay optimized and aligned with Google’s evolving algorithms, leading to better visibility and sustained traffic.
