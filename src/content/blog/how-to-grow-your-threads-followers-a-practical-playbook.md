**Quick answer:** No official Meta playbook for Threads growth turned up in the sources checked for this guide. What independent marketing guides agree on is cross-promotion from an existing audience, replying inside bigger conversations, and consistent posting. They disagree on exact cadence, and some even cite different monthly active user figures for the platform, so read any specific number with that in mind.

**Best for:** brands and creators starting or restarting a Threads account who want a realistic plan rather than a growth-hack list.

## What sources agree on

- **An existing audience elsewhere makes Threads much easier.** Guides repeatedly note that growth is significantly harder with zero starting audience, and recommend telling your Instagram, X or email audience that you are now posting on Threads.
- **Replies matter as much as original posts.** A reply to a larger, relevant account's post is seen inside that post's own reply thread, which is a bigger stage than most new accounts get from their own posts alone.
- **A consistent format beats a random one.** Accounts that settle into a recognizable kind of post (a daily tip, a running series, a particular voice) are described as growing faster than accounts posting whatever, whenever.
- **A clear hook in the first line matters**, the same principle that applies to ranking in AI search: say the interesting thing immediately, don't build up to it.

## Where sources disagree

- **Posting frequency.** One widely cited figure is 3-5 posts a week; another source recommends 3-7 posts a day for new accounts. These cannot both be optimal for the same account. Treat cadence as something to test on your own account rather than copy exactly.
- **Platform size.** Guides in this space cite monthly active user numbers ranging from roughly 200 million to 450 million for Threads in 2026. That is too wide a range to be the same figure measured the same way, so don't repeat either number as settled fact without checking Meta's own investor disclosures first.
- **Growth claims.** Case studies citing extreme percentage growth (such as a 30,000% figure) typically come from a very small starting base over a short window. Read the base number before treating a percentage as representative of what you should expect.

## A weekly routine to actually test this

1. **Pick one cadence you can sustain for four weeks** — daily is a reasonable starting default if you are not sure, since under-posting is the more common failure mode for new accounts.
2. **Post one original idea per day and reply to three relevant larger accounts per day.** Track which of the two drove more profile visits using Threads' own insights.
3. **Reuse your best-performing format.** If a specific kind of post (a short take, a numbered list, a quick how-to) consistently gets more replies, do more of that, not more of everything.
4. **Point your existing audience at the account explicitly** — a single announcement rarely does it; mention it again over a few weeks in whatever channel already has your attention.
5. **Review after four weeks**, not after three days. Social growth guides often promise fast results; judge your own account's early posts as a baseline, not a verdict.

## A separate question: ranking vs. growing

Growing followers and getting a post cited in Google AI Mode or AI Overviews are different goals that sometimes pull in different directions — a post written to be self-contained for AI extraction is not always the most "repliable" post for in-app growth.
