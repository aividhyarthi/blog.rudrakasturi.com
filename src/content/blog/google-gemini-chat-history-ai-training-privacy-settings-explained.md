Google is changing the privacy settings in its Gemini app. The update will let users decide separately whether their chats are saved in history and whether their chat content is used to train Google's generative AI models.

Until now, one setting called **Keep Activity** controlled both choices. Google is replacing it with two settings, giving people more control over how they use Gemini.

The change is useful for anyone who wants to keep a record of conversations but does not want those conversations used to train generative AI models.

There is one important detail, though. Turning off the new History setting will not mean that every trace of a conversation disappears immediately. Google's notice says future chats will still be saved for around two months for specific service, feedback and safety purposes.

## What is Google changing in Gemini?

Google says the existing Keep Activity setting will be replaced by two separate controls.

**1. History setting**

This controls how long Gemini chat history is saved. Users will be able to choose how long history is retained, use an auto-delete period and review or delete saved history manually.

**2. AI model training setting**

This separately controls whether chat content, including text and attachments, is used to train generative AI models.

The important difference is that users will be able to keep their chat history while switching off the use of that content for generative AI model training.

## Why is this change useful?

Imagine that you use Gemini to plan a project, research a subject or prepare a work document. You may want to return to the conversation a few weeks later because it contains useful information.

But you may not want the text of those conversations or the files you uploaded to be used to train Google's generative AI models.

Earlier, the Keep Activity setting combined these choices. The new arrangement separates them.

It gives users a more practical choice: retain conversations for personal reference while independently controlling whether their content is used for model training.

## Does turning off Gemini History delete your conversations immediately?

No. This is the most important qualification in Google's announcement.

Google says that when the new History setting is turned off, future chats will still be saved for around two months. The company says this temporary retention helps Gemini respond to users, process feedback and protect Google, its users and the public. Google says human reviewers may help with these purposes.

This means that turning off History should not be understood as a zero-retention setting.

It is important to distinguish between two things:

- **Not using chats to train generative AI models** is a specific control over model training.
- **Not retaining chats at all** would mean that the service does not keep the information after processing.

Google's announcement describes the first control separately, but it also explicitly describes a period of temporary retention when History is off.

## What happens to your existing Gemini settings?

Google says the new controls will inherit the current Keep Activity choice when the change reaches an account.

| Current Keep Activity setting | New History setting | New AI training setting |
|---|---|---|
| On | On | On |
| Off | Off | Off |

This is worth checking. If Keep Activity is currently on, Google says both new settings will be on when the change is made. Users who want to retain history but opt out of model training will need to review the new controls and change the training preference.

Google says the rollout will begin in the coming weeks. The settings may not appear for every user at the same time.

## Are audio and Gemini Live recordings included?

Google's notice says audio and Gemini Live recordings will not be used to improve Google services by default, as before. The company also provides separate information about controls for audio and Gemini Live recordings.

Users should not assume that the new chat history and text-and-attachment training controls automatically change every other Gemini data setting. Review the relevant controls in the Gemini app and the official Privacy Hub.

## What should Gemini users do?

When the new settings appear in your account:

1. Open Gemini settings and find the History or data and privacy controls.
2. Decide how long you want your conversations to remain available.
3. Check the separate control for using chat content, including text and attachments, to train generative AI models.
4. Review the settings for audio and Gemini Live recordings separately.
5. Revisit the Privacy Hub if you use connected apps or share sensitive files with Gemini.

The exact menu labels may change as Google rolls out the update.

## Is this a complete privacy solution?

It is a meaningful improvement in user choice, but it is not a guarantee that conversations are never retained or reviewed.

The new controls answer two different questions: how long should chat history be saved, and should chat content be used for generative AI model training?

They do not remove the need to understand the service's operational retention, safety processes, human review provisions or other connected Google services.

People should also avoid sending passwords, private keys, confidential customer records or sensitive business documents to any AI service unless they understand the relevant terms and have approval to do so.

## The bigger message: AI privacy settings are becoming more specific

As people use AI assistants for work, research, personal planning and file analysis, a single on-or-off privacy switch is not enough.

Users need to understand the difference between saving a conversation, using it to improve a model, processing it to provide a service and retaining it for safety purposes.

Google's Gemini update is a step towards making those choices clearer. The real benefit will depend on whether users notice the new settings, understand what each one controls and choose the configuration that matches their needs.
