Teams use AI to draft, research and structure content. Google's guidance (last updated 1 October 2026 as we read it) does not ban that. It sets two conditions: accuracy and added value.

**Best for:** content leads and editors who use AI tools and need a standard review before publishing.

![Checks for AI-assisted content: fact-check, review metadata, tell readers how it was made, do not mass-produce pages](/images/using-generative-ai-content/ai-content-checks.svg)

## What Google says

- Generative AI can help with researching a topic and adding structure to original content.
- Using it to generate many pages without adding value for users may violate the spam policy on scaled content abuse.
- Generative models do not retrieve facts; they predict a likely sequence of words, so outputs may contain inaccuracies. Google calls manual fact-checking and review critical before publishing.
- That review also applies to title elements, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text.
- Sharing how content was created gives readers context, for example how automation was used.
- Merchant Center has its own policies: AI-generated images need the IPTC `TrainedAlgorithmicMedia` metadata, and AI-generated product titles and descriptions must be specified separately and labelled.
- The Search Quality Raters guidelines (sections 4.6.5 and 4.6.6) describe how raters evaluate scaled content abuse and low-effort main content. Google says they are not a ranking guide.

## What to do: pre-publish checklist

| Step | Owner | Time | How to check | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Trace every claim to a source | Editor | 20 min per article | Open each source and compare | Each number and quote has a link |
| 2. Check names, dates and prices | Editor | 10 min | Compare against primary pages | No unverified specifics remain |
| 3. Review hidden elements | SEO | 10 min | Read the title, meta description, alt text and markup | None contains unchecked AI text |
| 4. Add first-hand value | Subject expert | 30 min | Does the page contain something only you could write? | At least one original example, test or number |
| 5. Disclose where readers would expect it | Editor | 5 min | Is the role of AI clear where it matters? | A plain sentence or note is added |
| 6. Run the scale test | SEO lead | 15 min | How many similar pages exist? | Near-duplicates are merged or dropped |
| 7. Log the process | Editor | 5 min | A shared record of who checked what | The log is saved with the draft |

## Common mistakes

- Checking the body but publishing an unchecked meta description.
- Letting a tool write structured data that does not match the page. See [structured data policies](/structured-data-policies-what-gets-your-markup-ignored/).
- Treating disclosure as a replacement for quality.
- Publishing hundreds of near-identical pages. Our [spam policy guide](/google-spam-policies-in-plain-english-a-self-audit-for-your-site-in-four-groups/) explains the policy in plain words.

## Our view

Use AI for structure and first drafts if you want, but let a named person own accuracy. Pair this checklist with [first-hand evidence](/first-hand-evidence-eight-kinds-you-can-add-to-any-page-and-what-never-to-fake/), which is what separates a page from a summary.
