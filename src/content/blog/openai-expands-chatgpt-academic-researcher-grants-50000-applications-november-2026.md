OpenAI is opening a new application round for its ChatGPT for Academic Researchers programme on November 23, 2026. The company plans to provide 50,000 new grants to eligible researchers, with each grant covering one researcher and up to two collaborators.

The programme provides free access to ChatGPT Plus. OpenAI says the broader initiative aims to reach 200,000 researchers worldwide.

The application round is being offered on a first-come, first-served basis rather than through the lottery system used during the pilot.

## What is being offered?

| Programme detail | OpenAI's announcement |
|---|---|
| New grants | 50,000 |
| Access per grant | Recipient plus up to two collaborators |
| Product access | ChatGPT Plus |
| Selection | First 50,000 eligible applications |
| Application opening | November 23, 2026 |
| Wider programme goal | 200,000 researchers worldwide |
| Data use | OpenAI says programme data is not used to train its models by default |

OpenAI says the grants will allow participants to use its frontier models and receive training on how to use ChatGPT in research.

## How did the programme reach this stage?

OpenAI says the programme began as a pilot in July 2026. Since then, 20,000 researchers across 33 countries have joined.

The company says it reviewed feedback and usage from the pilot before changing the next round. It found that most participants would have remained within ChatGPT Plus usage limits, so it chose Plus for the new grants rather than the Pro access used in the pilot.

The company also says it is moving away from lottery-based selection. Eligible applications will be considered in the order they are received until the 50,000 grants are allocated.

## What does a grant cover?

The grant is designed to support a small research team rather than a single account holder.

| Person covered | Access |
|---|---|
| Main applicant | Free ChatGPT Plus |
| Collaborator 1 | Included |
| Collaborator 2 | Included |
| Maximum people per grant | 3 |

OpenAI says the next round could therefore support up to 150,000 researchers through the 50,000 grants. That is the maximum implied by the grant structure, not a confirmed number of individual applications or active users.

## What kinds of research could use the programme?

OpenAI says different scientific fields will need different kinds of support. Its announcement describes research uses including exploring possible drug targets, and says it will continue learning from participants about the capabilities and support they need.

The programme is intended for academic research. It should not be confused with a general consumer subscription giveaway or a grant of research funding.

## What applicants should verify

The announcement says applications open on November 23 and that eligible applications will be handled on a first-come, first-served basis. Researchers should consult the official programme page for eligibility rules, application instructions and any regional or institutional conditions.

## Why this matters

The programme is an attempt to make advanced AI tools available to a larger research community. Its scale is notable, but the offer is access to a software product and training, not direct funding for laboratories, salaries or experiments.

The programme's impact will depend on who qualifies, how researchers use the tools and whether the access translates into useful and reproducible research outcomes.
