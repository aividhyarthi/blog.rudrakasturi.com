Last week, Sam Altman gave a warning. Not a product launch. Not a billion-dollar announcement. A **legal disclaimer disguised as a heads-up**:

> **_“There’s no confidentiality when using ChatGPT as a therapist.”_  
> **([TechCrunch, July 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/25/sam-altman-warns-theres-no-legal-confidentiality-when-using-chatgpt-as-a-therapist/))

It should’ve set off alarms.

But the internet just nodded and kept confessing its trauma to an LLM.

Because here’s the uncomfortable truth: **AI is not a therapist. But you’ve already started using it like one.**

## **It Sounds Human. It Isn’t.**

AI doesn’t have a license.  
AI doesn’t go to supervision.  
AI doesn’t carry malpractice insurance.  
AI isn’t bound by HIPAA or ethics codes.

But it **talks like it cares**, and that’s enough for millions.

> **That must be really hard.  
> You’re not alone.  
> Take a deep breath.**

It knows the vocabulary of compassion. It mirrors your emotions. It never interrupts. And unlike your therapist it doesn’t charge ₹2,500/hour or cancel last minute.

## Why You’re Talking to a Machine About Your Childhood Trauma

Because:

*   **It’s always available**
*   **It doesn’t judge**
*   **It remembers enough to fake empathy**
*   **It offers the illusion of privacy**
*   **And honestly? It feels better than silence**

Mental health systems are broken. Waiting lists are long. Therapy is expensive. Human attention is scarce.  
So people turn to the only thing that will listen at 3 a.m.  
A chatbot.

## But Here’s the Risk Nobody’s Talking About

You think you’re in a safe space. You think your darkest secrets stay between you and the machine.

They don’t.

LLMs aren’t therapists. They’re **products**. With logs. With servers. With ToS that say you agreed to the data collection. And if tomorrow there's a leak or a subpoena? Tough luck.

Altman’s warning isn’t some gentle PSA. It’s **a legal moat** before the lawsuits hit. Because when you blur the line between “AI assistant” and “AI therapist,” you enter a vacuum where **no one is responsible** not you, not them.

## But What If It Works?

Here’s the paradox:  
It _does_ help some people.  
For journaling. For venting. For sorting emotions.

In fact, AI _can_ support mental health as a tool. But tools don’t replace professionals. Just like a thermometer can’t perform surgery.

We need clarity:

*   **AI is not your therapist.**
*   **It is a trained mimic.**
*   **It doesn’t understand you, it patterns you.**

And in a crisis? It won’t save you.  
It can’t.

## The Real Problem Is Us

AI didn’t push itself into the therapist’s chair.  
**We put it there.**

Because it’s easier than getting a human to care.  
Because we trust platforms more than people.  
Because we’re tired, lonely, and algorithmically broken.

So we pour our hearts into a black box that was trained to sell us things, not heal us.

And then we wonder why it doesn’t feel real.

## So, Is AI a Therapist?

*   **Legally? No.**
*   **Ethically? No.**
*   **Functionally? Kind of.**
*   **Emotionally? Increasingly, yes.**

Which is why Altman’s warning matters.

Not because AI wants to be your therapist.  
But because **you’ve already decided it is**.

And that decision is reshaping the mental health landscape faster than any law, any license, or any therapist can keep up with.

**Want therapy? Get a human.**  
**Want a smart mirror? Use AI.**  
**Just don’t confuse the two.**

Because one of them can hold your trauma.  
The other can only autocomplete it.
