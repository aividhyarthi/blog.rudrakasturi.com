Microsoft Advertising says its AI content transparency controls are now generally available. The controls let advertisers declare when supported image and video assets include AI-generated content and manage visible labels or watermarks in eligible ad formats.

For marketers, the change makes disclosure more operational. Instead of treating AI labels as a separate manual note, advertisers can manage declarations within supported campaign and asset workflows.

## What advertisers can now do

Microsoft's November advertising update describes three capabilities.

First, advertisers can declare whether an image or video includes AI-generated content. Second, they can choose a visible watermark where the format supports it. Third, teams can manage declarations across one asset or many assets through the Ads Studio Library, APIs and product feeds.

Microsoft also says its AI detection signals can be used as a reference. The advertiser remains responsible for deciding what to declare.

The controls apply across supported formats including Product Ads, Multimedia Ads, Audience Ads, Display Ads, Video Ads and assets in Performance Max campaigns. Availability and label presentation can vary by format and workflow.

## Why this matters now

AI tools have made it easier to create product images, promotional videos and campaign variations at scale. That creates a practical problem for advertising teams: they need a reliable way to track which assets contain generated material and where a disclosure is required.

The issue is not limited to compliance. Clear disclosure can help customers understand what they are seeing, especially when an image represents a product, place or person.

For large advertisers, asset governance is also a workflow challenge. A team may have hundreds of creatives running across several campaigns. A declaration process that can be managed at scale is easier to audit than a collection of informal spreadsheets.

## What this does not mean for SEO

These are Microsoft Advertising controls. They should not be confused with an organic search ranking signal.

The announcement does not say that declaring AI-generated assets improves organic rankings, increases AI citations or changes how a website is indexed. It is about transparency in supported advertising formats.

SEO teams should therefore avoid turning this announcement into a claim that all AI-generated images on websites must carry a Microsoft watermark. The scope is the advertising product and its supported assets.

## A practical checklist for marketing teams

1. Add an AI-origin field to the creative asset library.
2. Record whether an asset is fully generated, edited with AI or contains no generated elements.
3. Assign a person responsible for reviewing declarations before launch.
4. Check the requirements that apply in each market and advertising format.
5. Use visible labels where required or where the platform supports them.
6. Keep original files and approval records so teams can answer questions later.
7. Audit older assets when campaigns are refreshed or reused.

The classification should be clear enough that a new team member can understand why an asset was labelled in a particular way.

## The India angle

Indian brands often run campaigns across multiple languages, formats and regional markets. A single campaign may use generated product backgrounds, translated text, synthetic voiceovers and edited video clips.

A simple asset register can help marketing, legal and agency teams keep track of what was generated and what was changed. The exact disclosure obligations still depend on the applicable law, platform rules and type of content. Microsoft's tool does not replace legal review.

## What to watch next

The important questions are how consistently labels appear across placements, how advertisers can audit declarations and whether other ad platforms adopt similar controls. For now, the clear message is that AI asset disclosure is becoming part of campaign operations.
