If you’ve ever used SEMrush or other SEO tools, you might have come across the term “search ranking volatility.” This term is often accompanied by notifications like “Volatility is High!” But what does that mean, and why should you care? Let’s break it down in simple terms.

### **What is Search Ranking Volatility?**

Search ranking volatility refers to how much website rankings fluctuate over a certain period. Normally, a website’s position in search engine results (like Google) remains relatively stable. However, when rankings move up or down drastically, we call this a "volatile" period.

Imagine it like the stock market—on some days, stock prices jump around a lot. Similarly, some days, search rankings can be unpredictable and show lots of movement.

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Screenshot-2024-10-14-at-8.12.42 AM.png)

### **Why Does SEMrush Warn About High Volatility?**

When SEMrush says "search ranking volatility is high," it’s a heads-up that search engine algorithms may be undergoing changes or there’s some significant shift in the search results. High volatility means you’ll likely see bigger jumps or drops in your rankings.

Think of it like weather forecasting. Just as a meteorologist might tell you a storm is coming, SEMrush is telling you the search results landscape is undergoing changes, and you should prepare.

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Screenshot-2024-10-14-at-8.14.14 AM-1024x989.png)

### **Why is Search Volatility Important?**

High volatility can be both good and bad. It signals that the search algorithm is in flux, meaning:

*   Your website’s ranking could improve.
*   Or, your website could drop in rankings.

The changes affect everyone, not just your site, so it’s important to be aware of what might be causing it and what actions you can take.

### **What Causes Search Ranking Volatility?**

### **Frequent Algorithm Tweaks by Google**

Google makes hundreds of changes to its search algorithm every year—sometimes even multiple times a day. Most of these changes are small tweaks designed to improve the search experience. Because these changes are minor, Google doesn’t announce or confirm them unless they are part of a big update, like a 'Core Update.'

Think of it like this: You’re always adjusting your car’s side mirrors for a better view, but you don’t talk about it unless you’ve made a big change like replacing the whole mirror! Google’s daily tweaks are those small mirror adjustments—hard to notice, but they keep the system running smoothly. So, if you notice your rankings fluctuating a little, it’s often just part of these minor, routine changes.

1.  **Algorithm Updates**: Google and other search engines frequently tweak their algorithms to improve how search results are ranked. Major updates like Google’s Core Updates can cause a lot of volatility.
    *   **Example**: When Google rolls out an update to focus on user experience (like page speed or mobile-friendliness), sites that haven’t optimized for these factors may drop, while well-optimized sites might rise.
2.  **Seasonal or Industry Changes**: Certain industries see more volatility during specific times of the year. For example, e-commerce sites experience ranking shifts around Black Friday or Christmas.
    *   **Example**: If you run a travel blog, you might see a spike in volatility around holiday seasons when people search for travel deals.
3.  **Competitor Actions**: Competitors might change their SEO strategies, which can cause shifts in the rankings.
    *   **Example**: If a competitor launches a new content strategy or secures high-authority backlinks, they might start ranking higher, causing you to move down in rankings.
4.  **User Behavior Shifts**: Changes in how people search or the type of content they engage with can also lead to volatility.
    *   **Example**: During the pandemic, there was a sudden surge in searches related to remote work, causing previously low-ranking content on that topic to jump up in rankings.

### **How Should You React to High Search Ranking Volatility?**

#### 1\. **Monitor Your Rankings**

First, keep an eye on your rankings. SEMrush can help you track your keyword rankings over time. Sudden drops or jumps during high volatility periods are common, but it’s essential to monitor them closely.

#### 2\. **Don’t Panic!**

It’s easy to get worried when you see your rankings drop during a volatile period, but remember—volatility affects everyone. The key is not to make hasty changes unless you have solid data on what’s causing the shifts.

#### 3\. **Focus on SEO Fundamentals**

While algorithms may change, certain SEO fundamentals remain critical. Ensure your website:

*   Provides high-quality, relevant content.
*   Is optimized for mobile and fast loading times.
*   Has a good internal linking structure and strong external backlinks.
*   **Example**: If you lose rankings during a volatile period, instead of panicking, focus on making your site faster and more user-friendly—two key factors search engines value.

#### 4\. **Evaluate Algorithm Changes**

If volatility persists, research if there’s been an algorithm update and whether it affects your industry or site type.

*   **Example**: After Google’s "Helpful Content" update, websites with thin or low-value content saw drops, while those focusing on comprehensive, user-friendly content gained rankings.

### **Final Thoughts**

High search ranking volatility is a signal that things are shifting in the world of SEO. Rather than fearing it, view it as an opportunity to analyze your site, improve your SEO strategies, and prepare for new ranking dynamics. SEMrush’s volatility alerts help you stay ahead of the curve, ensuring you can respond smartly and effectively.

**Takeaway: Don’t Make Hasty SEO Changes During Volatility**

One of the most common mistakes SEO professionals or business owners make is to panic during periods of high search ranking volatility. They might be tempted to overhaul their entire SEO strategy in a rush to recover lost rankings. However, making drastic changes during these fluctuations can cause bigger problems in the long run.

Once the volatility settles, your rankings may have recovered naturally. But if you made big, reactive changes, it could harm your overall SEO performance and take even longer to fix. The best approach is to stay calm and make informed decisions by consulting with SEO experts. This way, you’re adjusting based on data rather than reacting to temporary changes.

Stay informed, focus on your SEO fundamentals, and adapt when necessary—but don’t overreact to temporary ranking fluctuations!
