YouTube is making it easier for creators to manage their ad settings directly within the YouTube platform, without needing to go through AdSense. This new feature introduces **channel-level ad blocking controls**, and it’s designed to give creators more flexibility and control over the types of ads that appear on their content.

### How Does It Work?

YouTube is moving these ad controls to **YouTube Studio** on desktop, making it easier for creators to access them. Here’s a breakdown of how it works:

1.  **Where to Find the Controls**: Creators can find these ad-blocking options under the **Settings** section in YouTube Studio.
2.  **Who Can Use It**: These controls are available to channels that have **watch page monetization** enabled. This means if your videos are already making money through ads, you’ll have the option to customize which ads are shown.
3.  **What It Allows**: Creators can now block certain types of ads from appearing on their channel, helping them manage how their content is monetized.

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/image-64-1024x581.png)

### Why Is This Important?

This change can have a big impact on **brand safety**. It allows creators to decide what type of ads are appropriate for their content. For example, if a creator doesn’t want certain ad categories (like gambling or alcohol) to appear on their channel, they can block those types of ads. This can help ensure that the ads align with their content and audience values.

However, there’s a trade-off. **Blocking certain ad categories could reduce your revenue**, since fewer ads will be eligible to run on your videos. It’s a balance between ensuring your content aligns with your brand and maintaining the revenue your channel generates.

### The Potential Downside

YouTube has warned that restricting certain types of ads can lead to a drop in earnings. This is because fewer ads will be available to show on your videos, and in turn, this can also affect **Google’s ad revenue**. So while these new controls offer more power to creators, they should be used carefully to avoid a significant hit to income.

### Why Has YouTube Made This Move?

For years, YouTube has been taking steps to reduce the use of ad blockers, which were causing legal concerns. This new change gives creators **direct control** over their advertising settings, which can improve their satisfaction and ensure brand safety. By moving this functionality from **AdSense** directly into **YouTube Studio**, it streamlines the process for creators.

### What’s Next?

This feature will be rolling out by the **end of this week**. It marks a major shift in how creators can manage their ad settings and make decisions that are best for their channel’s content and brand.

### The Bottom Line

While these new ad-blocking controls offer creators more freedom, it’s important to consider the potential **impact on revenue**. Creators will need to find the right balance between protecting their brand and audience, and maintaining the income from ads. There’s no one-size-fits-all solution, so creators should evaluate what works best for their content and their financial goals.
