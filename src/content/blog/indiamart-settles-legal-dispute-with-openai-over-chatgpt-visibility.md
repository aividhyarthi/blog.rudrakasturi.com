For businesses, visibility in an AI answer can increasingly influence whether a customer discovers a marketplace, product or service. But what happens when a company believes it is being excluded from those answers?

IndiaMART's parent company InterMESH has settled its legal dispute with OpenAI over the alleged exclusion of its marketplace and seller listings from ChatGPT-generated responses.

Inc42 reported the development on November 26. The Calcutta High Court recorded that the parties had reached an out-of-court settlement and disposed of the appeal. The order did not disclose the settlement terms.

## What was the dispute about?

IndiaMART had alleged that ChatGPT was selectively excluding links to its marketplace while surfacing competing B2B marketplaces and individual seller websites.

The company approached the Calcutta High Court in December 2025. It argued that OpenAI was relying on the US Trade Representative's Notorious Markets List, which had raised concerns about counterfeiting and piracy involving IndiaMART.

IndiaMART argued that the US agency's findings were not legally binding in India and that OpenAI had relied on the report without giving the company an opportunity to present its position.

The company said the alleged exclusion caused reputational damage, loss of goodwill and commercial injury.

These were IndiaMART's allegations. They should not be presented as findings established by the court.

## What happened in the court?

In May 2026, a single judge of the Calcutta High Court rejected IndiaMART's application for interim relief.

According to Inc42's account, the court held that IndiaMART had not established a prima facie legal right to demand visibility on ChatGPT. It also observed that a private platform could not simply be compelled to display or promote another company's links.

IndiaMART challenged that decision before the appellate division.

An order dated November 17 recorded the submission that the parties had reached an out-of-court settlement and that IndiaMART no longer wished to pursue the appeal. The appeal was dismissed as not pressed and the connected application was disposed of.

The terms of the settlement were not made public.

## Does the settlement mean IndiaMART will appear in ChatGPT again?

There is no public confirmation of that.

The court order, as reported by Inc42, records that the parties settled. It does not say that OpenAI agreed to restore IndiaMART's visibility, change its source-selection systems or alter any marketplace policy.

That distinction is important. A settlement can resolve a dispute without creating a public precedent or disclosing the commercial or technical commitments made by either party.

## Why this matters for AEO and AI Search

The dispute raises a larger question for businesses: how much control do they have over their visibility in AI-generated answers?

Traditional search has established systems for crawling, indexing, ranking and reporting. AI answer platforms may select sources using a combination of retrieval, relevance, safety and product-specific systems.

A company may be indexed on the open web and still not appear in a particular AI answer. Visibility can vary by query, context, platform and the sources selected for a response.

Our [guide to tracking exact URLs cited by AI platforms](/what-are-cited-urls-ai-search-track-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity-2026/) explains why brands should measure actual citations rather than assume that website rankings automatically translate into AI visibility.

## What businesses should do

1. **Monitor brand and product prompts.** Test a fixed set of queries across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI experiences.
2. **Record citations and omissions.** Save the exact response, date, location and cited URLs.
3. **Check source accessibility.** Review whether important product and marketplace pages can be crawled and understood.
4. **Strengthen evidence and identity.** Keep company information, policies, product details and seller information clear and consistent.
5. **Separate visibility from ranking.** A brand mention, a cited URL and a referral visit are different outcomes.
6. **Avoid assuming a legal remedy guarantees visibility.** Platform selection systems and commercial policies may be separate from the specific dispute.

A useful metric is *AI citation coverage*: the percentage of a defined set of relevant commercial prompts in which a brand or its pages appear as a cited source. Track it separately from brand mentions and referral traffic.

## The commercial question behind the legal dispute

The IndiaMART case is important because it brings a practical AEO concern into a legal setting. Businesses increasingly care about whether AI platforms include them in answers that influence buying decisions.

But visibility in an AI response is not the same as a guaranteed listing position in a traditional search engine. The settlement closes this particular dispute; it does not settle the wider debate about how AI platforms should select, explain and report their sources.
