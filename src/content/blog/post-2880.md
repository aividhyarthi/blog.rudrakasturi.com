Let’s talk about something very real and currently affecting a huge chunk of the web: Google seems to be **indexing a lot less content than usual** since the end of May 2025.

If you’re a site owner, SEO, blogger, or content strategist—and you’ve noticed that pages that used to get indexed quickly are now sitting in limbo in Search Console—you’re not alone. This isn’t just a glitch in your matrix. This is happening to hundreds, possibly thousands of sites.

Let me break it all down—what’s happening, why it _might_ be happening, what Google has (and hasn’t) said about it, and what you should do right now if your content is suddenly being ignored by Googlebot.

### What’s the Issue?

Since around May 27–29, 2025, webmasters across the world have started reporting a sudden spike in pages being crawled but not indexed. Most of the complaints are about this status showing up in Google Search Console:

> **“Crawled - currently not indexed”**

This means:

*   Google’s bot did crawl your page.
*   But it decided not to index it (for now, or maybe ever).

Now, if you’ve worked with SEO long enough, you know this happens from time to time. But the **scale** of reports and the **timing** make this stand out. This doesn’t seem to be a small hiccup. It looks more like a deliberate shift or experiment in how Google is approaching indexing.

### What the SEO Community Is Seeing

SEOs across platforms like Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and Google Search Central have been talking about this. Some agencies and professionals with high-authority clients are seeing thousands of pages being left out of the index, even when:

*   The pages are high-quality.
*   They’re submitted in the sitemap.
*   They have internal and external links.
*   There’s no technical issue.

Even well-established content that would’ve been indexed within a day or two just a month ago is now being ignored.

### Google’s Response (So Far)

So far, Google hasn’t issued any official blog post or announcement confirming a change to indexing behavior. But **John Mueller**, Google Search Advocate, did respond to some of the questions on Bluesky and Twitter.

Here are a couple of direct quotes from him:

> **“Which millions of pages? Happy to hear specifics.”**  
> “We don't index all content, and what we index can change over time.”

So Google’s not denying it—but they’re also not calling it a bug. What they are saying, in their usual understated way, is: indexing is not guaranteed. And it never has been.

### Why Is Google Indexing Less?

There’s no official explanation from Google. But based on what we’re seeing and how Google typically handles these situations, here are some likely reasons:

#### 1\. Google is Reacting to AI-Generated Content Overload

Let’s face it—since late 2023, the internet has been flooded with AI-generated content. Some of it is good. Most of it is not. Google’s crawlers are probably overwhelmed by the sheer volume of repetitive, low-quality material.

This might be Google tightening the quality filters. In other words, if your page feels generic or mass-produced, it might get crawled but left out of the index.

#### 2\. Your Crawl Budget May Be Getting Recalculated

Google doesn’t crawl and index everything. Every site has a crawl budget—how many pages Googlebot is willing to fetch per day or week. If you suddenly start publishing more content than usual (especially low-quality or similar-looking content), Google may crawl it but decide to hold off on indexing most of it.

Sites with technical problems, thin content, or low authority could get deprioritized.

#### 3\. Indexing Experiments or Algorithm Shifts

Google is constantly running experiments. It could be testing new indexing systems, delaying indexing for lower-authority pages, or using engagement signals to decide what’s worth including in the index.

Also, keep in mind: this comes just weeks after several algorithm updates and the expansion of AI Overviews. There may be cross-effects we’re not seeing yet.

### What You Should Do Right Now

Here’s what I’d recommend if you’re seeing a big drop in indexing:

#### 1\. Check "Crawled - Currently Not Indexed" Pages in Search Console

Open Search Console, go to “Pages” under Indexing, and filter to show “Crawled - currently not indexed.” Study what kinds of pages are affected.

Ask yourself:

*   Is this content thin or repetitive?
*   Is it useful to users or just filler?
*   Does it have internal links pointing to it?
*   Is it included in the sitemap?

You may start seeing a pattern.

#### 2\. Improve or Remove Low-Value Pages

Google might be silently raising the bar. If a page is weak, duplicated, or shallow, it’s probably not going to make it in.

What you can do:

*   Beef up the content
*   Add relevant internal links
*   Combine it with other similar content
*   Or, if it’s not valuable at all—just delete it

Focus on quality over quantity.

#### 3\. Tighten Internal Linking

Pages with no internal links or buried deep in your site hierarchy are less likely to be indexed.

Make sure every page you care about:

*   Has at least 2–3 internal links
*   Is included in menus, footers, or content hubs
*   Is in your XML sitemap

Also check that those links aren’t blocked by robots.txt or using “nofollow.”

#### 4\. Use Manual Indexing (Selectively)

For critical content—like a product page, lead-gen page, or news article—you can try a manual index request in Search Console.

But don’t abuse this. It doesn’t guarantee inclusion. Googlebot will re-crawl the page, but indexing is still its call.

#### 5\. Don’t Panic—but Monitor Closely

Some pages take longer to index now, especially for newer or lower-authority sites. Don’t assume something’s wrong right away.

But if this is a broad, ongoing issue across your site, it might be time to re-evaluate your entire content strategy.

### My Thoughts

This sudden drop in indexing is frustrating, especially since Google isn’t giving clear answers. But it might also be a wake-up call.

Here’s the truth: indexing is no longer just about submitting URLs and waiting a few hours. It’s becoming more selective. Google has to decide what's worth including in its ever-growing index, and the standard is rising.

That means we all need to get back to basics:

*   Create useful, original content.
*   Improve technical SEO.
*   Focus on what your audience actually needs.

If you're building fast, shallow, and high-volume content farms, this new behavior might hit you the hardest.

But if you’re publishing thoughtful, well-structured, high-quality pages that serve real user intent, you’re still playing the right long game.

Let’s not forget: Google’s mission is to organize the world’s information—not to index every page we throw at it.

Keep building value.  
Keep watching your data.  
Keep evolving.
