Google Search Central added support for Things to Do queries to its aggregator and supplier unit documentation on 9 November 2026.

The update matters to travel and experience platforms that help users discover activities, attractions and bookable experiences. It is not a promise that every travel website will receive a new Search feature or ranking boost.

![Travel discovery flow: user searches for an activity, eligible aggregator and supplier information can help discovery and comparison](/images/news/google-things-to-do-search-flow.svg)

## What changed

Google's documentation update says aggregator and supplier units now support Things to Do queries. Aggregator experiences bring together options from multiple providers. Supplier experiences represent the business or provider offering the activity.

## Who should pay attention

- Travel marketplaces listing tours, attractions and activities.
- Booking platforms that aggregate multiple suppliers.
- Individual experience providers with eligible inventory.
- Technical SEO teams maintaining structured data and feeds.

A general travel blog should not assume that it qualifies simply because it writes about things to do.

## What businesses should check

1. Read Google's current aggregator and supplier unit documentation.
2. Confirm whether the business model and inventory match documented eligibility.
3. Review required structured data, inventory information and landing page quality.
4. Ensure availability, prices and provider details are accurate.
5. Monitor Search Console and referral traffic after implementation work.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean Google has announced a universal new ranking factor for travel content. It does not guarantee a rich result or traffic increase. It does not remove the need for useful landing pages and accurate booking information.

## Our analysis

The update is a reminder that Search is not only a list of webpages. It can also surface structured experiences that help users move from discovery to comparison and booking. For travel platforms, the opportunity is to make inventory and provider information understandable to Google's systems while keeping the user experience accurate.
