#### **What’s Changed?**

Google updated its policy on **[site reputation abuse](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#site-reputation)** to clarify that using third-party content to exploit a site’s ranking signals is against the rules. This applies even if the site owner is not directly involved in the content's creation or oversight.

#### **What is Site Reputation Abuse?**

This refers to the practice of hosting **third-party content** on a website to **manipulate search rankings**, taking advantage of the site’s established reputation and authority.

#### **Examples of Site Reputation Abuse:**

1.  **Off-topic Content Hosting:**
    *   A **medical site** hosting a third-party page about "best casinos."
    *   A **sports site** hosting pages about "workout supplements reviews."  
        _Why it's abuse:_ These pages are irrelevant to the site's core purpose and exist solely to exploit its rankings.
2.  **Confusing User Experience:**
    *   A **movie review site** featuring articles on "how to buy followers on social media."  
        _Why it's abuse:_ Such topics are misleading for users and intended to game search rankings.
3.  **Third-party Pages to Rank Irrelevant Content:**
    *   An **educational site** publishing payday loan reviews created by a third party.  
        _Why it's abuse:_ It exploits the site's authority to rank unrelated content.
4.  **Coupons for Rankings:**
    *   A **news site** publishing third-party coupons only to capitalize on its reputation.  
        _Why it's abuse:_ The content serves no real editorial purpose and is meant to manipulate rankings.

#### **What to Do if You're Affected**

If your site has such pages, **exclude them from Search indexing** to avoid policy violations.

#### **What’s Allowed Under the Policy?**

Some practices are **not considered site reputation abuse**, including:

*   **Wire services** or **press releases**.
*   **News syndication** where content comes from trusted sources.
*   **User-generated content** on forums or comment sections.
*   **Editorial content** like opinion pieces or articles with clear purposes.
*   **Affiliate links** or embedded ad units, provided they are used appropriately.

#### **Why Does This Matter?**

Google’s update ensures that search results remain **relevant and trustworthy**, preventing websites from using third-party content to manipulate rankings unfairly.

For more information, check Google's [blog post detailing the policy changes and their implications](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#site-reputation).
