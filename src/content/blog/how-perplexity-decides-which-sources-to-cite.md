When you ask Perplexity a question, it does more than return a list of web pages. It searches for information, analyses sources and presents an answer with links that allow readers to check where the information came from.

For publishers and SEO professionals, this creates an important question: what makes one website appear as a citation while another relevant website is left out?

Perplexity explains some parts of its search process publicly. However, it does not publish a complete ranking formula that tells website owners exactly why every source is selected. Understanding the documented process is more useful than following unverified claims about how to get cited.

## How Perplexity searches for information

Perplexity describes Pro Search as a process involving several stages.

### It searches across the web

Perplexity's help documentation says Pro Search conducts multiple searches across sources such as articles, academic papers, forums and videos, depending on the selected focus.

This is important because a question does not always need one type of source.

For example, someone asking about a new government policy may need an official notification. Someone comparing a consumer product may need manufacturer specifications, independent reviews and user experiences.

### It analyses and combines information

Perplexity says it reads, analyses and compiles information from sources to produce a structured answer.

This means that the final response is a synthesis, not necessarily a direct reproduction of one article.

A website may provide one important fact while another source provides background or a different perspective.

### It provides links to sources

Perplexity says its answers include direct links to original sources so that users can verify information or explore further.

This is an important distinction between an answer that simply makes a claim and one that gives the reader a way to check it.

## Why might Perplexity cite one website instead of another?

The complete selection formula is not public. Therefore, website owners should not treat any single SEO technique as a guaranteed citation factor.

There are, however, practical questions worth examining.

### Does the page directly answer the question?

If someone asks "What is the minimum investment required to buy US stocks from India?", a page explaining the general history of the US stock market may not be the most useful source.

A page that clearly explains account requirements, minimum order values, fractional shares and applicable restrictions may be more relevant.

The answer should appear clearly in the content, with enough explanation and evidence to support it.

### Is the information current?

Some questions are time-sensitive.

Product pricing, government rules, company information and technology specifications can change. A page that has not been reviewed for several years may contain outdated details.

Update the information when something changes. Do not change the publication date simply to make an old page look new.

### Does the page support its claims?

A page making a claim should provide evidence where necessary.

For example:

- A financial claim should link to relevant filings or official disclosures.
- A medical claim should refer to suitable clinical or public health sources.
- A product specification should be checked against manufacturer documentation.
- A legal explanation should point readers towards the relevant law or official guidance.

A source list should not be added merely for decoration. The linked source must actually support the statement.

### Can Perplexity access the page?

A page cannot be retrieved if the relevant system cannot access it.

Check server responses, robots.txt rules, page rendering, internal links and any access restrictions.

Also remember that crawling permission is not a promise of citation. Making a page accessible only removes one possible technical barrier.

## How to check whether Perplexity is citing your website

Choose 10 to 20 questions that your website is designed to answer.

For each question:

1. Open Perplexity and enter the exact question.
2. Record the answer and every cited URL.
3. Open the cited pages.
4. Check whether each page actually supports the answer.
5. Note whether your website appears.
6. Repeat the test on another date.

For example, an SEO website could test:

- How does Google Search Console measure search performance?
- What is the difference between GEO and AEO?
- How can a website improve its AI search visibility?
- Does Google use llms.txt?
- How do AI search platforms cite websites?

Avoid changing the wording between tests if you want to compare results.

## What should you do if Perplexity does not cite your website?

First, identify the pages it is citing instead.

Compare those pages with your own article.

| What to compare | What to check |
|---|---|
| Answer relevance | Does the competitor answer the question more directly? |
| Information quality | Does it provide better explanations and evidence? |
| Original information | Does it contain research, examples or first-hand experience? |
| Freshness | Is the information more current? |
| Structure | Can a reader find the answer easily? |
| Source quality | Does it link to credible primary sources? |
| Technical access | Can crawlers access and retrieve the page? |

Use the findings to improve the actual content gap.

For example, if a competitor's guide explains five practical steps and your article only defines the topic, adding a useful process may improve the value of your page.

Do not copy the competitor's content. Explain the subject in your own way and provide evidence for your own claims.

## Does Perplexity prefer certain websites?

There is no public evidence that supports a universal rule such as "Perplexity always prefers news websites" or "Perplexity always cites Reddit first".

Its documentation describes searching across different types of sources, depending on the question and selected focus.

The source that is useful for a medical question may not be useful for a software comparison. A government source may be essential for a regulatory question, while a product review may be more relevant for a buying decision.

The right approach is to study the sources that appear for your specific topic rather than assume one type of website always wins.

## A simple Perplexity citation tracking sheet

Track the following metrics monthly:

| Metric | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Citation frequency | Number of tested prompts where your URL appears |
| Domain visibility | Number of tested prompts where your domain appears |
| Citation relevance | Relevant citations divided by total citations |
| Competitor visibility | Number of prompts where competitor domains appear |
| Source accuracy | Whether cited pages support the claims |
| Referral visits | Visits received from Perplexity, where analytics identifies them |

For example, if your website appears in 5 of 20 tested prompts, your observed citation frequency is 25%.

This is a private monitoring metric. It is not a score published or endorsed by Perplexity.

## What matters more than chasing citations?

A citation is useful because it gives a reader a path to the original information. But a citation by itself does not prove that a page is authoritative or that the answer is correct.

Publish information that is clear, current and supported by evidence. Make sure important pages are technically accessible. Review the actual questions your audience asks and improve pages that do not answer them adequately.

That is a more dependable strategy than trying to reverse-engineer a ranking formula that Perplexity has not disclosed.
