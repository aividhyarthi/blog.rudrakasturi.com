Featured snippets can assist increase brand awareness and organic traffic. This step-by-step guide will show you how to optimise for them. It's no secret that featured snippets may be extremely effective. Every SEO practitioner (including me) strives to own as many highlighted snippets as possible for their content. 

These enlarged, descriptive search results are displayed in a separate box near the top of the search results page (SERP). When you optimise for featured snippets (FS), Google will be able to tell when your page is the best answer for a relevant query with one of these search features available. 

The expanded snippets that appear first in Google's result pages are known as Featured Snippets. The goal of Google featured snippets is to provide answers to users' questions right in the search results. 

**As Google defines, “Featured snippets** are selected search results that are **featured** on top of **Google's** organic results below the ads in a box. **Featured snippets** aim at answering the user's question right away (hence their other well-known name, "answer boxes").” 

When Google first introduced this feature, certain websites were able to get two results on page one of the SERPs, resulting in a significant spike in traffic organically. This entire process is a lot more complicated than adding queries to your headlines and subheadlines for featured snippets.  

When it comes to optimising for featured snippets, it's important to know what types of snippets are available. To begin with you can target four different types of featured snippets. 

1.  Featured Snippets (paragraph): The paragraph type accounts for 70% of featured snippets, with an average of 42 words and 250 characters.
2.  Listicle Featured Snippets: Listicle style snippets account for 19% of all highlighted snippets, with an average of 6 items and 44 words.
3.  Tables make up about 6.3 percent of featured snippets. They feature five rows and two columns on average, each with 40 to 45 words.
4.  Snippets from the Video: Only 4.6 percent of highlighted snippets are videos, with the average running time of 6 minutes and 35 seconds.
