If you're a news publisher, blogger, or someone running a content site in India, here's something you need to know about Google's new AI Mode.

It doesn't just show links anymore.

It now **summarises the news** for users, and adds **source names** in two different styles. But here's the twist - only one of them might help you get actual traffic.

Let’s break it down.

![Screenshot of an online article discussing the Ellenbarrie Industrial Gases IPO, detailing its Grey Market Premium (GMP) fluctuations, trends, expected listing price, subscription status, and key takeaways.](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Screenshot-2025-06-26-at-11.13.08 AM-1024x691.webp)

### **1\. The “Footnote” Style - Seen but Not Clicked**

Let’s say someone searches for _“Ellenbarrie IPO GMP”_ on Google in AI Mode.

Google shows a neat paragraph explaining what happened. At the end of that paragraph, it adds this:

> **Source: The Economic Times**

This is a **footnote-style credit**. It tells the reader where the info came from, but doesn’t shout it out.

The problem? Most users read the summary and move on. They **don’t click**.

So while your name is there, your site doesn’t get much out of it.

### **2\. The “Inline” Style - The One That Matters**

In some searches, Google adds the source **inside** the paragraph, like this:

> **According to _The Economic Times_, the IPO saw a drop in GMP from ₹26 to ₹18.**

This is called **inline linking**. It’s more powerful because:

*   It stands out more
*   It makes your brand look trusted
*   Users are **more likely to click**

But here’s the catch - not every site gets this treatment. Google seems to **pick and choose** who gets inline mentions.

### **What Decides Whether You Get an Inline Link or a Footnote?**

Google hasn’t told us the full formula, but based on what we see:

*   Trusted brands (like ET, NDTV) get more inline credit
*   Structured, cleanly written content is more likely to be picked
*   Timely reporting still helps, but it’s not enough

In short - **writing fast isn't enough. Writing clean and credible matters more**.

### **For Indian Publishers: Why This Matters Right Now**

AI Mode is live in India.

That means users are starting to see **summaries instead of 10 blue links**.

If your brand is only showing up in the side panel or as a quiet footnote, you’re not really in the spotlight.

In some cases, **smaller Indian publishers who actually broke the news** are missing completely, while **global sites** (like CNBC or Wikipedia) get featured.

So it’s no longer just about SEO or breaking a story first. It’s about making sure **Google can understand, summarise, and highlight your content**.

### **What Should You Do Now?**

Here’s your simple checklist:

*   **Use your brand name** in your articles clearly
    *   **Example: “According to NDTV reports...”**
*   **Write clean, structured content**
    *   Avoid messy formats or long paragraphs
*   **Add proper headings and schema**
    *   These help Google figure out what your story is about
*   **Track AI Mode as much as Search**
    *   If you're only looking at clicks from regular search, you’ll miss what’s going on in AI Mode

### Why You Should Care?

You may still rank well in Google Search.  
But in AI Mode, **you have to earn your mention**.

And once you get it, make sure it's the kind that **puts your brand in the story**, not just as a small note at the bottom.

This is not just a tech change. It’s a shift in how visibility and trust are rewarded in the AI era.

**_Start adapting now - because in this new mode, it’s not just about what you say. It’s about how Google says it for you._**
