When Google announced support for social and video platform properties in Search Console, most people saw another product update.

I saw something very different.

Google quietly changed who Search is built for.

For nearly 25 years, Google Search revolved around websites.

If you wanted visibility, you built a website.

If you wanted traffic, you invested in SEO.

If you wanted revenue, you ranked on Google.

Creators played a completely different game. They chased followers, subscribers, watch time and engagement on platforms like YouTube, Instagram and TikTok. Publishers chased rankings, backlinks and keywords.

The internet had two economies.

The Search economy.

The Creator economy.

Google has just started merging them.

That may sound dramatic, but look closely at what happened.

Google is no longer measuring only websites.

It has started measuring creators.

That changes far more than Search Console.

It changes how influence, authority and discoverability will be measured in the future.

## The Invisible Wall Between Search and Social Has Finally Started Cracking

![](https://blog.rudrakasturi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-998x1024.webp)

For years, creators lived inside platforms.

Instagram knew how successful an Instagram creator was.

YouTube knew how successful a YouTuber was.

TikTok knew how successful a TikTok creator was.

Google knew almost nothing.

If someone asked a creator, "How many people discovered you through Google Search?" there was no meaningful answer.

That wall has now started to disappear.

Creators can now understand how their content is discovered through Google Search, even if they don't own a traditional website.

At first glance, that sounds like a reporting feature.

It isn't.

It is Google recognising creators as independent publishers.

That recognition is far more important than the dashboard itself.

## This Is Bigger Than Search Console

Think about Google's mission.

"To organize the world's information."

For years, Google assumed most valuable information lived on websites.

That assumption is no longer true.

Today, some of the best tutorials exist on YouTube.

The best travel recommendations often come from Instagram creators.

Financial explainers are increasingly published as short videos.

Product reviews begin on social platforms before they appear on blogs.

The internet changed.

Google had to change with it.

Instead of asking people to create websites, Google is adapting Search to wherever useful content already exists.

That is a massive philosophical shift.

Google isn't indexing websites anymore.

It is indexing expertise.

## Google Is Quietly Building a Creator Graph

![](https://blog.rudrakasturi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-1-1024x680.webp)

We often talk about Google's Knowledge Graph.

Now it feels like Google is building something equally important.

A Creator Graph.

Imagine a future where Google understands:

Who creates trustworthy content.

Which creator consistently answers questions about finance.

Who is an expert in skincare.

Who publishes the best electric vehicle reviews.

Who explains AI better than everyone else.

Search becomes less about domains and more about identities.

If that happens, creators become searchable entities rather than just social accounts.

The latest Search Console update feels like one of the first visible steps in that direction.

## The Real Winner Isn't the Creator. It's Search.

Every Google update solves Google's own problem first.

This one is no different.

People no longer consume information the way they did ten years ago.

A teenager looking for skincare advice may never visit a blog.

They watch a Reel.

Someone repairing a washing machine searches YouTube.

Someone learning Excel watches Shorts.

Someone planning a holiday trusts travel creators more than travel websites.

If Google ignored those behaviours, Search would slowly become less useful.

So instead of competing with creators, Google is bringing them into Search.

That makes Search more complete.

It also makes Google harder to replace.

## This Changes the Rules for Creators

Until yesterday, creators sold reach.

"I have one million followers."

"I generated five million views."

"I reached two million people."

Tomorrow, another metric enters the conversation.

"I generated eight million Google Search impressions."

That matters.

Followers tell brands how many people subscribed.

Search impressions reveal how many people actively searched for a topic and discovered that creator.

Those are very different signals.

One measures popularity.

The other measures demand.

Brands understand the difference.

## Advertisers Just Got a Better Way to Judge Influence

Influencer marketing has always had a measurement problem.

Follower counts can be inflated.

Views fluctuate.

Engagement varies wildly.

Search data introduces another layer.

Brands can now ask questions they couldn't ask before.

How often do people discover this creator through Google?

Which topics drive search visibility?

Does this creator own a category in Search?

Imagine comparing two creators.

One has two million Instagram followers.

Another has two lakh followers but appears in millions of Google Search impressions every month for investment advice.

Which creator would a financial services company trust more?

The answer may surprise many marketers.

## Publishers Should Be Paying Attention

Most publishers will celebrate this update.

They shouldn't celebrate too quickly.

This update also creates competition.

For years, publishers competed mainly with other publishers.

Now they compete with creators whose content may answer the same questions in a more engaging format.

A cooking article now competes with a 60 second recipe video.

A gadget review competes with a creator's hands on demonstration.

A financial guide competes with an explainer Reel.

Search is becoming platform neutral.

The best answer wins.

Not necessarily the best website.

That should make every newsroom rethink its content strategy.

## The Rise of Creator SEO

For two decades, SEO largely belonged to websites.

The next decade may belong to creators.

Titles will matter.

Captions will matter.

Video descriptions will matter.

Transcripts will matter.

Entity recognition will matter.

Creators who understand search intent will build long term visibility instead of relying only on algorithmic reach inside social platforms.

A new discipline is emerging.

Creator SEO.

And it won't stay niche for long.

## My Predictions

Here are a few predictions I believe we'll see over the next few years.

Google will expand Search Console support to more creator platforms as the creator ecosystem grows.

Brands will begin asking creators for Google Search performance alongside platform analytics during campaign negotiations.

Influencer pricing will increasingly reflect search visibility, not just follower counts.

Media companies will hire creators who understand Search intent, not just journalists who understand SEO.

Agencies will start offering Creator SEO services, helping influencers optimise titles, captions, transcripts and content structure for Google Search.

The line between publisher and creator will continue to disappear.

## Final Thoughts

Many people will remember this as the day Google added another report to Search Console.

I think history will remember it differently.

This is the moment Google officially acknowledged that expertise no longer lives only on websites.

It lives with creators.

With journalists.

With educators.

With analysts.

With storytellers.

Search is evolving from an index of websites into an index of people, ideas and expertise.

For creators, this means visibility beyond social platforms.

For advertisers, it means better signals to identify real influence.

For publishers, it means stronger competition from individuals who understand both storytelling and Search.

The biggest mistake anyone can make is to treat this as a feature update.

It isn't.

It's Google quietly redefining what deserves to be discovered.

For twenty five years, Search belonged to websites.

The next chapter may belong to creators.

And years from now, we may look back at this announcement as the moment Google quietly changed the rules of the internet once again.
