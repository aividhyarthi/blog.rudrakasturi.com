Karnataka has signed memorandums of understanding with five technology companies to expand artificial intelligence training for students and working professionals. The partnerships target 63,200 people across the state and were announced during Bengaluru Tech Summit 2026.

ETGovernment reported that the partners are Google, Salesforce, Pega Systems, Sarvam AI and Gnani.ai. The programme is intended to expose engineering and diploma students, along with working professionals, to generative AI, agentic AI, AI agent development, voice AI, enterprise platforms, cloud computing and data analytics.

The number is a training target, not a claim that 63,200 people have already completed a course or received a certification.

## What will the programmes cover?

According to the report, the initiatives are expected to combine structured learning with practical exercises, hands-on work with industry tools, capstone projects and certification pathways. Gnani.ai has a separate partnership with the state's Department of Electronics, IT and Biotechnology to train 1,000 engineering and science students in multilingual voice AI applications and AI agents.

The emphasis on voice AI is relevant to India, where public services, education and business applications need to work across languages and levels of digital literacy. But the value of training will depend on whether learners build usable projects and can demonstrate skills to employers.

## Why the announcement matters

India's AI talent discussion is moving beyond awareness sessions and basic prompt writing. Employers increasingly need people who can work with data, evaluate model outputs, connect tools, build workflows and understand security and privacy.

A state-level programme can widen access to industry exposure, especially for students outside the largest technology companies. Partnerships with vendors may also give learners access to current platforms and practical instruction.

## How to judge whether the programme succeeds

The headline target is only the first metric. Karnataka should also report:

1. Enrolment and completion separately.
2. Number of learners who build and demonstrate a working project.
3. Participation by district, institution and gender.
4. Certification pass rates and employer recognition.
5. Internships, placements or measurable workplace outcomes after training.
6. Whether course material is updated as AI tools change.

These measures would help distinguish training activity from actual skill development.

## The next challenge

India has no shortage of AI courses. The harder task is turning classroom exposure into practical ability. If the partnerships produce local-language applications, useful enterprise workflows and stronger entry-level job readiness, the programme could have an impact beyond its enrolment target.

**Bottom line:** Karnataka's five-company partnership targets 63,200 learners. The real measure of success will be how many complete the training, build working solutions and use those skills in employment or business.
