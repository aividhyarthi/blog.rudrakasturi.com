Google Search advertising is moving into a more conversational environment. Instead of responding only to a short query such as "best laptop under ₹70,000", AI Mode can handle a longer request, compare several requirements and continue the discussion through follow-up questions.

Google is now bringing ads into that experience in more markets, including India. On 28 October 2026, the company announced that ads in AI Mode were expanding to Australia, Canada, India, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore.

The announcement also introduced multiturn ads in the United States, allowing ads to appear in follow-up queries in AI Mode. This is an important distinction: the international expansion and the US multiturn launch are related developments, but Google did not say that multiturn ads were launching in every new market at the same time.

For advertisers, the change is not simply another place to display a text ad. It is a move towards matching advertising with the context of a longer customer conversation.

## What is conversational advertising in Google AI Mode?

AI Mode is Google's AI-powered Search experience for more complex questions and exploratory tasks. A user can ask a detailed question, review an answer and continue with follow-up questions without starting a completely new search each time.

Google says queries in AI Mode are, on average, three times longer than traditional searches. That figure comes from Google's own research, cited in its October announcement.

Consider someone planning a family holiday. The first question might ask for destinations within a particular budget. The next could ask about travel time, child-friendly accommodation, weather and activities. The user's requirements become clearer as the conversation develops.

In a conventional search journey, advertisers might target several separate keywords. In a conversational experience, the system can interpret the context across the interaction and identify when a sponsored result may be relevant.

Google says ads can appear within or alongside AI-generated responses when a search or conversation has commercial relevance. The placements are labelled "Sponsored" and visually distinguished from organic content.

## What did Google announce on 28 October?

Google's announcement contains two developments.

**1. Ads in AI Mode expanded to ten markets.** The markets named by Google include India, Australia, Canada, Kenya, Malaysia, New Zealand, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Singapore.

**2. Multiturn ads launched in the US.** Google said it was launching ads in follow-up queries in AI Mode in the United States. The announcement did not say that this specific multiturn capability was launching in all ten expansion markets.

Google also said it plans to expand to additional markets in Q4 2026, naming Brazil, Mexico and the United Kingdom. Treat future market plans as plans until Google confirms availability.

## How is this different from traditional Search advertising?

| Traditional Search | Conversational AI Mode |
|---|---|
| Often begins with a short keyword query | Can begin with a detailed, multi-part question |
| Advertisers plan around keywords, match types and intent | The experience can interpret context across a longer interaction |
| Users often open new searches to refine their needs | Users can ask follow-up questions within the same experience |
| Ads appear in established Search placements | Sponsored content can appear within or alongside AI-generated responses |
| Search terms and campaign reports provide familiar signals | Advertisers must also understand how campaigns perform in evolving AI placements |

This does not mean keywords have disappeared. Google's own guidance continues to position traditional Search campaigns as an important way to capture high-intent demand. AI Mode adds another environment in which relevant ads may appear.

## What does this mean for advertisers in India?

India's inclusion in the expansion is significant because businesses can now prepare for AI Mode advertising as part of the changing Search landscape. However, availability of an ad experience in a country does not guarantee that every advertiser, campaign or query will receive a placement.

The likely areas of impact include:

**Ecommerce.** A shopper may describe a product need through budget, size, features and use case. Accurate product feeds and clear product information become important inputs to the overall shopping experience.

**Travel and hospitality.** People often refine a trip through several constraints, such as dates, family size, location and budget. Advertisers should ensure that landing pages answer the practical questions raised by those searches.

**Education.** A prospective student may compare courses, eligibility, fees, location and placement information. Enquiry quality matters more than raw form volume.

**Financial services.** Users may explore loan eligibility, repayment, fees and product differences. Advertising claims and landing-page information need to be especially clear and compliant.

**Local services.** A customer may move from describing a problem to comparing providers and service areas. Accurate location, service and availability information can help avoid mismatched enquiries.

These are potential use cases, not evidence that each sector is already receiving a measurable performance lift from AI Mode ads in India.

## What should Google Ads teams prepare?

**1. Keep Search campaigns healthy.** AI Mode does not replace the need for relevant keywords, strong ad assets, sensible bidding and clear campaign structure.

**2. Review AI-powered campaign options.** Google highlights Performance Max and AI Max for Search as part of its AI advertising toolkit. Check current campaign eligibility in Google Ads Help and your own account rather than assuming every format is available to every advertiser.

**3. Improve product and business information.** For ecommerce, maintain accurate Merchant Center feeds, availability and pricing. For services, make the offering, service area and eligibility clear on the landing page.

**4. Strengthen conversion tracking.** Distinguish a form submission from a qualified lead, sale or subscription. Where appropriate, use CRM feedback and offline conversion imports so bidding systems can learn from outcomes that matter to the business.

**5. Prepare for longer, more specific intent.** Review search terms and conversion paths. Understand which questions customers ask before they become ready to buy, and ensure the landing page answers the next practical question.

**6. Monitor placement and performance carefully.** Record what is available in your account, compare campaign performance over meaningful periods and avoid attributing a change to AI Mode without evidence.

**7. Keep brand claims consistent.** AI-generated experiences can summarise products and services. Make sure your website and other public information accurately explain what you offer and what you do not.

## Does Google say these ads influence organic AI answers?

No. Google's Ads Help documentation says ads in AI-powered Search experiences are labelled as sponsored and separated visually from organic content. It also states that paid advertising does not influence the organic responses generated by Google AI.

Advertisers should not treat buying ads as a shortcut to being cited in an AI answer. Paid visibility and organic citation are separate outcomes and need separate measurement.

## What we still need to learn

Google's announcement provides the market expansion and describes its direction for conversational advertising. It does not provide a complete public breakdown of performance by country, industry, campaign type or conversion quality.

The company says click-through rates for sponsored and organic product list items in AI Mode were similar in its US internal data from 20 August to 3 September 2026. That is Google's own short-period US data, not an independent benchmark and not a promise of similar results in India.

Advertisers should wait for account-level evidence before shifting budgets on the assumption that AI Mode will outperform standard Search.

## Is this the next big shift in search advertising?

It could become an important change in how Search ads meet customer intent. The traditional model often starts with a keyword and a landing page. Conversational AI creates a longer interaction in which the customer's needs can become clearer over several questions.

That gives Google more context for matching sponsored results, but it also raises expectations. The ad, product information and landing page must fit what the customer is actually trying to do.

For advertisers, the practical response is to prepare the fundamentals: clean feeds, accurate information, meaningful conversion signals and landing pages that answer real customer questions. The new format may change where ads appear. It does not remove the need to prove that the resulting traffic is valuable.

## The takeaway

Google expanded AI Mode ads to India and nine other markets on 28 October 2026. It also announced multiturn ads for follow-up queries in the US. The development points towards a more conversational form of Search advertising, where relevance can depend on the context of a longer interaction rather than a single short query.

Indian advertisers should understand the distinction between market expansion and the US multiturn launch, check current campaign eligibility and strengthen measurement before changing budgets. The opportunity is not simply to appear in another placement. It is to reach the right customer with the right information at the right point in a conversation.

*Editorial note: Market availability, campaign eligibility and ad formats can change. Google's official announcement and Ads Help documentation are the primary references for current details.*
