Crawl budget, also known as crawl space or crawl time, is a term used to describe the amount of time spent for crawling by the search engine. The amount of attention a web crawler pays to your site is referred to as the crawl budget. This degree of focus is determined by how often it actually wants to crawl and or how often does your website provide an opportunity to be crawled. Search engines won't be able to crawl your website efficiently if you waste crawl money, which will damage your SEO performance.

What does the term "crawl budget" mean in SEO?

Crawl budget is a phrase used by the SEO industry to describe a collection of linked ideas and methods used by search engines to determine how many and which pages to crawl. It's essentially how much attention search engines will pay to your website. To begin, we'd want to highlight that, as detailed below, crawl budget is not a concern for most publishers. Crawl budget isn't anything webmasters should worry about if new pages are crawled the same day they're published. Similarly, if a site contains fewer than a few thousand URLs, it will be crawled efficiently most of the time. 

However, the great majority of websites do not need to be concerned with crawl budgets. Google is quite adept at locating and indexing web pages.

Why do search engines give websites crawl budgets?

In a nutshell, if Google doesn't index a page, it won't be ranked for anything. So, if the quantity of pages on your site exceeds the crawl budget, you'll have pages that aren't indexed. This is made easier by allocating a crawl budget to each website.
