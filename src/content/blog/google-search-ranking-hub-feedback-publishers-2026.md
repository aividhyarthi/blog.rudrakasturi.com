When a website loses visibility, SEO teams often look for one place that explains what Google has changed and how to report a problem. Google has now brought several of its ranking resources and feedback routes together in a central Search Ranking Hub.

The launch is a documentation and communication change, not a new ranking algorithm. Its practical value is that publishers have a clearer route to official guidance and a better-defined way to report patterns that may point to wider problems.

The hub appeared in Google Search Central on November 24, 2026.

## What is the Google Search Ranking Hub?

The [Google Search Ranking Hub](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/ranking) is a central collection of resources about how Search ranks results and how website owners can keep up with changes.

Google says the hub brings together three broad areas:

- Its principles for ranking results.
- Guidance on staying informed about ranking updates and policy changes.
- Ways to submit feedback about ranking changes in Search.

It also links to information about how ranking works and what site owners can do when traffic changes.

The goal is to make information that was spread across different Search Central pages easier to find. It does not replace the Search Status Dashboard or the detailed documentation for individual ranking systems.

## What has Google said about its ranking principles?

The new page sets out three broad principles.

**Users come first.** Google says results are ranked according to their usefulness for the user's query.

**Consistent systems across result types.** Google says it does not design its ranking systems or AI models to favour the interests of a particular website.

**Rigorous testing.** Google says ranking changes are evaluated before they are introduced.

These are principles, not a list of ranking factors. The page does not disclose the exact weight of links, content, freshness, user signals or any other individual signal. It should not be treated as a new technical SEO checklist.

## How can publishers submit feedback about a ranking change?

Google has added a more structured route for feedback about ranking changes. The process can use a hashtag associated with a particular update, such as the hashtag provided for the November 2026 core update.

Search Engine Land reported that Google intends to review feedback in aggregate so its teams can identify possible systemic problems. That is different from asking Google to restore one website's traffic or manually reconsider one URL.

A useful submission should explain the issue clearly. Include the update involved, the affected page types, the dates when the change began, examples of queries and URLs, and evidence that the problem may affect more than one page or site.

Avoid sending a message that only says, "My traffic dropped after the update." A traffic decline is a symptom. Google needs enough detail to understand what may have gone wrong.

## What the hub does not change

The launch does not mean Google has made its ranking algorithm fully transparent. It does not publish the complete set of signals or their weights.

It does not guarantee that feedback will lead to a ranking change. Nor does it replace the work of diagnosing a technical issue, a content-quality problem, a manual action or a change in search demand.

The hub is a documentation and communication improvement. It is not a promise that every ranking fluctuation will receive an individual explanation.

## A practical response plan for SEO teams

When a website loses visibility, use the following sequence before submitting feedback.

1. **Check the dates.** Compare the traffic decline with confirmed rollout dates on the [Google Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/). Do not assume that every movement is caused by a core update.
2. **Separate Search surfaces.** Review Web Search, Discover, Google News and Search generative AI performance separately where the relevant reports are available.
3. **Find the affected page groups.** Segment by directory, template, content type, country, device and query intent. A site-wide average can hide the actual problem.
4. **Check technical changes.** Review deployments, robots.txt, canonical tags, redirects, noindex rules, structured data and server errors.
5. **Compare query demand.** A fall in clicks can happen even when rankings are stable if search demand or the result layout changes.
6. **Review the content.** Check whether pages answer the intended question, contain current information and offer evidence or experience that readers cannot get from a generic summary.
7. **Submit evidence-based feedback.** Use the update-specific route when the issue appears broader than one page. Include examples and the pattern you have identified.

For a detailed recovery process, use our guide to [diagnosing Search traffic drops after a Google update](/what-to-do-after-a-google-core-update-a-calm-seven-step-plan-for-seo-teams/). For AI discovery, keep a separate record of visible citations and referred visits rather than mixing them with ordinary organic clicks.

## How should publishers use the new hub?

Add the Search Ranking Hub to the team's reference list. Use it to check Google's stated principles, find official update information and understand the available feedback routes.

But do not change a website simply because a new documentation page has appeared. A central hub is useful for finding reliable information. It is not evidence that a ranking system has changed.

The practical gain is better diagnosis. Publishers can spend less time searching for scattered Google documents and more time identifying what changed on their own websites, which pages were affected and what evidence supports a possible explanation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Search Ranking Hub a new Google ranking algorithm?

No. It is a central documentation and communication resource. Google has not announced a new ranking system through the hub.

### Can publishers ask Google to restore a website's rankings?

The feedback route is not a manual appeal process for an individual website or URL. Google says feedback is reviewed in aggregate to identify broader issues.

### Does the hub reveal Google's ranking factors and their weights?

No. It explains broad principles and links to documentation, but does not publish a complete list of ranking signals or their weights.

### Where should SEO teams check whether a Google update is happening?

Use Google's Search Status Dashboard for confirmed incidents and rollout information, and use the Ranking Hub for the wider documentation and feedback resources.

### What should a publisher include in ranking feedback?

Include the update, dates, affected page types, example URLs and queries, and evidence of a repeatable pattern. A general statement that traffic fell is not enough to explain the issue.

### Will submitting feedback guarantee a ranking change?

No. Google has not promised an individual response or ranking change for every submission.
