A quiet revolution is happening in the way we search for information online. AI chatbots like Perplexity and ChatGPT are slowly but surely becoming the go-to tools for many tech-savvy users, challenging Google’s long-standing dominance. Why is this shift happening, and what does it mean for us, businesses, and even Google itself? Let’s break it down in a simple and easy way to understand.

### What’s the Big Deal About AI Chatbots?

#### **Faster and Direct Answers**

Think about how you search on Google: you type in a question, click on a few links, and hope to find the right answer. With AI chatbots, the answer comes to you directly. For example:

*   [Matthew Berman](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-10/early-adopters-are-ditching-google-search-for-ai-chatbots?srnd=undefined), an early user of Perplexity, found that it’s faster and less frustrating than Google. “It saves so much time,” he says. He has cut down his Google usage by **90%**.

#### **No More Link Clicking**

Chatbots like Perplexity and ChatGPT don’t just give you links—they summarize information from various sources and even provide the links if you want to dig deeper. This is why people like Matthew prefer them for things like planning a camping trip or answering random questions quickly.

### Challenges with AI Chatbots:

#### **Mistakes Happen (They Call It Hallucination)**

AI chatbots are not perfect. Sometimes, they give wrong or confusing answers.

*   Rebecca Shomair, who works at Cisco, used ChatGPT to ask for hiking spots near Palo Alto. The chatbot gave her a wrong map!
*   Users often compare chatbots to interns—you need to double-check their work.

#### **Trust Issues**

When you search on Google, you can evaluate websites yourself to see if the information feels reliable. With chatbots, it’s harder to know how accurate the information is unless they provide proper source links (which not all of them do).

### What’s Google Doing About It?

Google knows that chatbots are becoming popular, so they’ve started adding AI to their search results with a feature called **AI Overviews**.

*   These Overviews give quick answers at the top of search results and are already reaching **1 billion users every month**!
*   Sundar Pichai, Google’s CEO, says this feature is now available in more than **100 countries**, and users are happy with it.

But here’s the tricky part: Google makes a lot of money (over **$224 billion in ad revenue in 2022**) by showing ads in search results. If people stop clicking on search ads, it could hurt Google’s business.

### How Are AI Chatbots Making Money?

Right now, AI chatbots like Perplexity and ChatGPT don’t rely on ads like Google does. Instead, they charge users a subscription fee:

*   **$20 per month** for ChatGPT Plus or Perplexity Pro.
*   Hundreds of thousands of people are already paying for these subscriptions.

This means a big shift could be coming—people may get used to paying for smarter and faster searches instead of relying on free tools like Google.

### What Does This Mean for You and the Internet?

#### **For Users**

If you’re tech-savvy or in a hurry, chatbots are great for quick, direct answers. However, they aren’t perfect. It’s smart to double-check important information, especially if it’s about health, money, or anything critical.

#### **For Businesses**

Many businesses depend on Google for traffic. If chatbots become more popular, businesses will need to figure out how to appear in chatbot responses or risk losing customers.

#### **For Google**

Google is in a tough spot. It needs to stay innovative to compete with chatbots while also protecting its advertising business. At the same time, regulators are keeping a close eye on how Google uses AI, making it even harder for the company to make bold moves.

### Will Chatbots Replace Google?

Not anytime soon. Here’s why:

1.  **Google Has Scale**  
    With billions of users, Google still has a massive lead over chatbots like Perplexity, which had only **15 million users in March 2024**.
2.  **Chatbots Aren’t Perfect**  
    Their mistakes (hallucinations) can make them unreliable for now. Google’s AI Overviews might feel safer for everyday users.
3.  **Cost Matters**  
    Google is free. Chatbots cost $20/month. While early adopters are happy to pay, most people won’t spend money unless the service becomes indispensable.

* * *

### Final Thoughts

AI chatbots like Perplexity and ChatGPT are making waves, and early adopters are already changing their habits. While they’re fast and convenient, they’re not flawless. Google, on the other hand, is working hard to improve search with its own AI features, keeping itself relevant in this changing landscape.

As these tools evolve, we’ll all need to adapt. For now, the smartest approach might be to use both—chatbots for quick answers and Google for double-checking.
