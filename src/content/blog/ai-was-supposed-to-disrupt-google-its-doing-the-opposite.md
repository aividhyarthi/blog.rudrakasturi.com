A year ago, the prediction was simple. AI chatbots would replace search that is Google

Now the data is in. The outcome looks different.

In June 2025, ChatGPT and other AI tools sent **53.1 million visits to Google.com** and another **37.9 million to YouTube**. Together, that’s over **91 million referrals** in one month to platforms owned by the company everyone assumed would lose in the AI shift.

This isn’t disruption. It’s reinforcement.

### Where the Traffic Is Really Going

The referral data comes from [Similarweb](http://similarweb.com), which recently published rankings of the top 50 domains getting traffic from AI chatbots. The top two are not niche AI-native upstarts. They’re the same giants that have dominated internet discovery for two decades: Google and YouTube.

Even as AI chatbots grow rapidly, total referrals from AI to websites jumped **357 percent year-on-year** most of that growth is still flowing to the old internet. Not the new one.

With media and news sites alone seeing a **770 percent surge**. But even within that, Google and YouTube remain the largest recipients of traffic.

### The Feedback Loop

There’s a reason this is happening.

Chatbots like ChatGPT are not indexing the entire web on their own. They are still trained on links, citations, and examples from trusted sources. And when users ask for information that requires detail, credibility, or video ChatGPT links to Google, YouTube, Wikipedia, Reddit, or NIH.

AI doesn’t escape the structure of the old web. It depends on it.

Even Perplexity, a search-style AI engine, links back to source domains. And the sources with the most well-structured, widely linked, and highly trusted pages? Still Google-owned properties.

### Google and YouTube Are Still the Center of the Web

Whether it's ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude, AI platforms are still leaning heavily on Google’s ecosystem. Users may be asking questions inside ChatGPT, but the final answers often lead back to Google or YouTube.

*   For deeper research: ChatGPT links to Google Search results or support pages
*   For visual learning: AI chatbots suggest YouTube videos
*   For technical how-tos or cloud tools: Google Docs, Sheets, and developer pages still dominate

The result is that **Google remains the most-visited domain from AI referrals**, with YouTube close behind. These are not small numbers. These are tens of millions of sessions every month, flowing directly from AI interfaces.

### Not Just Search

The irony is sharper when you consider the AI narrative. These tools were built to reduce clicks, summarize everything, and keep the user inside the chat window. That’s not happening at least not yet.

Users still click. Chatbots still refer. And Google continues to benefit.

In fact, YouTube is gaining even more from this shift. Ask ChatGPT for a tutorial, review, or walkthrough, and the most common output is a YouTube link. Video wins where text still struggles. AI recognises that.

So does the user.

### The Bottom Line

AI hasn’t disrupted the internet’s traffic flows. It has just rerouted them through a different front door one where Google still sits at the back end.

There’s no question that AI chat is changing how discovery works. But the winners, so far, are familiar. Same domains. Same platforms. Same logic.

The conclusion is simple. For now, AI isn’t erasing Google. It’s feeding it. And Google is quietly collecting the traffic.
