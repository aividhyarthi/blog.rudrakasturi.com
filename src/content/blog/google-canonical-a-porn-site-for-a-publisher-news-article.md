Yes, this actually happened.

A well-known Indian news publisher wrote and published a proper, original article.  
Google crawled it... and then decided the **original version exists on a porn site**.

Result?  
Google **gave credit to the porn site**, not the publisher.  
That’s called “canonical” in Google terms – the version it prefers to show and rank.

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/image-22.webp)

## Just imagine that.

A real newsroom team worked hard – reporting, writing, editing, publishing.  
But when Google’s systems scanned the web, they got confused.  
And the scraper or porn site got all the credit.

This isn’t just a small technical issue.  
It’s the exact opposite of what Google keeps preaching about **trust, E-E-A-T, and helpful content**.

## What really happened?

*   A porn site copied the news article (either fully or partially)
*   Google crawled both the porn site and the original
*   Instead of choosing the publisher, Google thought the porn site was the “main source”
*   The publisher's article was marked as **duplicate**
*   And it disappeared from top Google Search results

This one small mistake by Google hits the publisher in a big way –  
They lose traffic, trust, and even future rankings.

## But wait – doesn’t Google say “E-E-A-T” matters?

Apparently not enough.

Here’s what actually happens when Google messes up canonical like this:

*   Traffic drops (especially in important morning hours)
*   Breaking stories don’t get seen
*   Publisher loses topical authority
*   SEO teams get blamed, even though it’s not their fault
*   And during a core update? The site can get hit badly or even penalized

## What can a publisher even do in this case?

Imagine this:

*   You’re already struggling to get Google to index your stories on time
*   Finally, it gets crawled – but Google thinks it’s copied
*   It sets the canonical to a random site
*   And your article disappears from Google

Even if you fix it 2 days later, the moment is gone.  
The story is gone.  
The traffic is gone.  
The trust is gone.

## What does Google really want?

You keep saying: “Write helpful, original content” we do that.  
You say: “Build E-E-A-T” Indian publishers have done that for years.

Still, a porn site gets picked over a news brand?

## This is not a one-time glitch.

This shows Google’s systems crawling, indexing, canonical setting are **not ready** for real-time news publishing. Especially in fast-paced markets like India.

## What needs to change?

*   Google should respect publisher authority when setting canonicals
*   Scraper sites must be _actively punished_, not rewarded
*   News crawl in India should be faster and smarter
*   There must be a way to fix mistakes _quickly_, not wait for months
*   And AI Mode or Search Updates shouldn’t destroy a site because of one backend error

## So,

If Google truly supports quality journalism and factual content then stop treating Indian news like a side project.

Because if a porn site can copy your story and still get credit, then all the talk about AI, quality content, and helpful results means _nothing_.
