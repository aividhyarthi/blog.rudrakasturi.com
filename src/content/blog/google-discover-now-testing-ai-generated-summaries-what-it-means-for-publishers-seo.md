**Spotted in the wild**: Google Discover appears to be **testing a new “AI summary” box** on top of news cards signaling a quiet but significant change in how mobile users engage with news and content.

![](https://rudrakasturi.wpcomstaging.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Discover-AI-Summary.webp)

Pic Credit: X.com platform

### What’s Happening?

A screenshot shows a **summary snippet placed right below the article title and source**, reading like a condensed version of the article possibly auto-generated. This test appears above selected cards on the Google Discover feed (for example, IGN’s _How to Train Your Dragon_ remake article).

**Key observations:**

*   The summary is separate from the article description.
*   It doesn't appear on all cards, only a select few.
*   It’s not authored by the publisher likely generated by Google's internal models.
*   There’s a “feedback” prompt asking: _“How would you rate this suggestion?”_ with emoji-based rating buttons.

### Why This Matters

This experiment, though subtle, has **huge implications for content creators, publishers, and SEO-driven websites**:

#### 1\. **Google Is Becoming the Content Layer**

By summarizing articles _before_ a user clicks, Google reduces the need to visit the original source much like AI Overviews in search. This could:

*   Lower click-through rates (CTR) from Discover.
*   Shift user intent toward “scrolling to skim” vs. “clicking to read.”
*   Compress publisher monetization windows (ads, subscriptions, etc.).

#### 2\. **Discover Could Become Another AI-Fueled Traffic Gatekeeper**

If these summaries get more accurate, they could:

*   Replace publisher headlines/descriptions with Google-curated blurbs.
*   Shape user perception of the story before the first click.
*   Prioritize _“quick value delivery”_ over full content consumption.

#### 3\. **Content Strategy Must Adapt**

Publishers can no longer rely only on:

*   CTR from snappy headlines or thumbnails.
*   Article intros as Discover bait.  
    Instead, they must consider:
*   Structuring content so summaries (AI or human) highlight key value early.
*   Testing how AI summarizes your headlines and adjusting language to guide outcomes.
*   Tracking CTR drop if summaries become default.

### Who Should Care?

*   **Media houses** relying on Discover for mobile traffic.
*   **SEO strategists** trying to future-proof their organic reach.
*   **Product teams** at content startups — this affects user behavior and funnel depth.

### Final Thought: Discover Is Becoming “Preview First”

Just like AI Overviews in Search and Summarized Results in YouTube Shorts/Google News, this test confirms that **Google’s long-term direction is preview-first, click-optional.**

**If you don’t control the preview, you don’t control the story.**
