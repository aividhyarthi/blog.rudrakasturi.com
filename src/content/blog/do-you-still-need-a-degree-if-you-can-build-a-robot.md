Last week, a student in China didn’t show up to his graduation ceremony.  
Instead, he sent a humanoid robot - built using AI and mechanical design skills to walk on stage, shake the principal’s hand, and collect his diploma.

It looked like a gimmick.  
But it’s really a mirror.

And what it reflects back is this:  
**If you can build a robot smart enough to graduate for you… do you even need a degree anymore?**

### Meanwhile, in India…

We are still:

*   Cracking our knuckles to fill OMR bubbles.
*   Standing outside coaching centres in Kota at 5 a.m.
*   Judging intelligence by cut-off scores, not capabilities.
*   Preparing for entrance exams like they’re war.

The Indian education system is built for filtering, not building.  
It rewards compliance, not creativity.  
It’s a machine designed to sort you into boxes not break them.

### Some Numbers to Ground the Reality

*   Over **9 lakh students** appear for **NEET** every year.
*   Nearly **11 lakh** sat for **JEE (Main)** in 2024.
*   Only **2.6%** made it to IITs.
*   And yet, India produces over **1.5 million engineers** annually, many of whom don’t get placed.

Compare that to a 17-year-old who didn’t fight to top a test.  
He built a robot.

He built something.

### The Big Question No One Wants to Ask

**What’s more valuable?**  
A 95% CBSE certificate? Or a robot that walks, talks, and collects that certificate for you?

What’s a better signal of future-readiness?

*   Solving 120 questions in 3 hours?
*   Or deploying an AI-enabled project to solve a real-world problem?

In India, the answer is still unclear. Because our system is built to honour rank, not relevance.

### Degrees Still Matter… But Their Power Is Cracking

Let’s be honest.  
In India, degrees are still:

*   Social status symbols.
*   Visa prerequisites.
*   Parental validation.

But step outside the country or even into the world of tech startups and AI innovation and the ground shifts. Fast.

Proof of Work > Proof of Marks.

The robot kid? He has a portfolio. Not just a certificate.

### What India Needs to Ask Now

If a student can:

*   Build an AI-powered robot,
*   Navigate hardware and code,
*   And demonstrate real-world thinking at 17…

Should he still be asked to fill MCQs for another 5 years?  
Should he still need a government-recognised piece of paper to prove his worth?

Or should we start building a new model, one where:

*   Creativity is scored.
*   Projects are credentials.
*   And the system _recognises output, not just obedience._

### In My View:

The Indian system didn’t break.  
It just stayed still while the world moved on.

One student sent a robot to get a certificate.  
The real flex?  
He didn’t _need_ it.
