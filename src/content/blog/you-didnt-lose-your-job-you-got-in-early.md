If you got laid off from SEO, here's the first thing I want you to do. Stop worrying.

Not because it doesn't hurt. It does. But because you're standing at the start of something most people never get to stand at the start of. The AI SEO world just began. You didn't miss it. You're early to it. Sit with that for a second, because most people go their whole career waiting for a moment like this and it never comes for them.

It came for you. This month.

## Learn it. Build it. Show it. Then go to market.

Most people get the order backwards. They go looking for the next job first, with nothing to show, just a resume and a hope that someone believes them.

Flip it. Learn the tool before you pitch it. Build something with it, even something small and rough. Show it publicly, let people watch you actually do the thing, not just talk about it. Only then go to market, whether that's a client, a company, or a room full of students. Learn, build, show, market. That order, every time.

## Where to put this once you've started

Learn, build, show comes first. Always. After that, here's where the energy goes.

**Consult instead of waiting to be hired.** Most companies don't need a full-time SEO on payroll right now. They need someone who already understands what changed and can walk in and fix it. Be that person, not the one waiting for a job listing to appear.

**Get fluent in AEO before it becomes a job title.** Figure out why ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity cite one brand over another. Almost nobody can explain this clearly yet. Be early to the explanation, not late to the trend.

**Build something, even small.** A tool, a plugin, a tracker, doesn't matter what. This is the one where the Java line matters most. You don't need years of coding to build with AI the way you needed years of coding to build with Java. That barrier is gone. Use the fact that it's gone.

**Teach while you're still learning.** Colleges and companies are scrambling to understand this and most of the people teaching it haven't actually done the work. You will have, in a few weeks, if you start now.

**Post it all publicly.** Every build, every test, every AEO experiment. This is how the work finds you, instead of you chasing it.

## Gen Z is already moving. You still have the edge.

Twenty two year olds with zero work experience are already building small AI tools, testing AEO, posting what they're learning, picking up their first clients. Nobody gave them permission. They just started.

Here's what you have that they don't. Years of actually sitting across from clients, fixing what's broken, explaining SEO to a CEO who didn't get it the first three times, being wrong and learning from it. That experience, combined with the same tools everyone has access to right now, is a real edge. Don't hand a twenty two year old a head start on something that's brand new for both of you.

## This is the opportunity, not the setback

The layoff didn't end anything. It removed the one thing that was keeping you comfortable, which was the excuse to keep moving at someone else's pace.

You get to start on your own terms now. Not eventually. Now.

## Java was born before you. AI was born in front of you.

Think about what that actually means.

Java existed before most of us touched a keyboard professionally. We learned it after millions of developers already had a head start. The ecosystem, the jobs, the frameworks, all built by people who got there before us. We were always catching up.

AI is being built right now, while you're reading this line. You're not late to it. You're watching it happen live, in real time, and you get to decide how early you walk in. This does not happen twice in a career. Don't treat this layoff like a setback when it's actually the one door that's still wide open.

**Learn it. Build it. Show it. Go**.
