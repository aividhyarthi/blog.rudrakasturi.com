Google is changing how people return to earlier searches. The company says it is globally rolling out a collapsible sidebar on its desktop homepage, giving users a way to find recent AI searches and continue exploring a topic.

The change may look like a small interface update. Its significance is that Google is making search history and ongoing AI conversations more visible at the point where people begin a search.

## What is changing

![Google AI Mode search results page with the new collapsible sidebar open, showing the "Close sidebar" toggle in the top-left corner next to the search tabs](/images/google-ai-mode-collapsible-sidebar/ai-mode-sidebar-screenshot.png)

Google's Robby Stein, vice president of product for Search, announced the rollout. The sidebar is designed to help users jump back into recent AI searches. Google says people increasingly arrive with questions that need more than one interaction, and that testing showed the sidebar helped users make progress and continue exploring.

The feature is a navigation aid. It makes previous AI Mode activity easier to revisit and gives users a more direct way to resume a line of research instead of starting again.

## Why Google is doing this

Traditional search often begins with one query, a list of links and a click. AI Mode supports a more conversational journey. A person can ask a broad question, narrow it down, compare options and follow up with another question.

If a user has to remember the exact wording or find an old browser tab to continue, the conversation can lose momentum. A persistent sidebar reduces that friction.

The design also signals that Google wants people to treat AI Mode as an ongoing search environment rather than an occasional feature hidden behind a separate button.

## What it means for SEO

This is not a confirmed ranking update. Google has not said that pages will rank higher because a user revisits an AI Mode conversation. Nor does the sidebar itself guarantee more clicks to publishers.

The practical implication is about user behaviour. Search journeys may become longer and more iterative. A person researching a product, a health topic or a business decision may return to the same thread several times, ask new questions and explore additional sources.

That makes it useful for publishers to think beyond a single keyword. A good content cluster should help a reader move from the first question to the next sensible question.

For example, a person searching for a home loan may move through eligibility, interest rates, documents, lender comparisons and repayment planning. Each page should answer its own question clearly and link naturally to the next step.

## What publishers should do

1. Build topic journeys around real reader questions, not just keyword variations.
2. Make every page useful when someone lands on it directly from an AI answer.
3. Use descriptive headings and answer-first introductions so the main point is easy to understand.
4. Link to the next relevant explanation without forcing the reader through unrelated pages.
5. Track branded searches, returning visitors, assisted conversions and referral traffic separately.
6. Review Search Console and analytics data over time rather than attributing every traffic change to one interface update.

## What not to do

Do not create dozens of near-identical pages in the hope of appearing in more AI conversations. Do not assume that the sidebar is a new ranking factor. And do not measure success only by whether a page receives a click from the first query.

The better question is whether your content helps someone complete the entire information journey.

## The larger shift

Google's sidebar makes a simple point visible: search is becoming less like a one-time question and more like a continuing task. For publishers, that raises the value of clear explanations, useful topic connections and pages that help people take the next step.

The interface has changed. The basic publishing requirement has not: give the reader something worth returning to.
