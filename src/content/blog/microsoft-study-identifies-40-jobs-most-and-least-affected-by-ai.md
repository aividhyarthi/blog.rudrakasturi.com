A new Microsoft Research study reveals which occupations are most exposed to artificial intelligence and which remain insulated for now. Drawing on over 200,000 interactions with Microsoft’s Bing Copilot, the analysis assigns an “AI applicability score” to more than 80 job roles, helping to forecast the evolving workplace landscape

### **Jobs Most at Risk: High “AI Applicability”**

Jobs that heavily rely on routine communication, content generation, or data processing scored highest:

*   Interpreters and translators
*   Historians
*   Passenger attendants
*   Sales representatives (services)
*   Writers and authors
*   Customer service representatives
*   CNC tool programmers
*   Telephone operators
*   Ticket agents and travel clerks
*   Broadcast announcers / radio DJs
*   Brokerage clerks
*   Farm & home management educators
*   Telemarketers
*   Concierges
*   Political scientists
*   News analysts / reporters / journalists
*   Mathematicians
*   Technical writers
*   Proofreaders and copy markers
*   Hosts and hostesses
*   Editors
*   Postsecondary business teachers
*   Public relations specialists
*   Demonstrators & product promoters
*   Advertising sales agents
*   New accounts clerks
*   Statistical assistants
*   Counter & rental clerks
*   Data scientists
*   Personal financial advisors
*   Archivists
*   Postsecondary economics teachers
*   Web developers
*   Management analysts
*   Geographers
*   Models
*   Market research analysts
*   Public safety telecommunicators
*   Switchboard operators
*   Postsecondary library science teachers

Many of these roles involve tasks that AI tools like Copilot and ChatGPT can already perform effectively, including summarization, automated responses, and language translation.

These jobs require physical presence, manual tools, or interpersonal interactions that current generative AI cannot replicate.

### **Why You Should Care?**

*   **AI is reshaping workflows, not eliminating professions - yet.** Microsoft emphasizes that these roles are being augmented rather than replaced. AI is a productivity tool, not a replacement solution
*   **Adaptability and upskilling are critical.** Workers in high-overlap roles are advised to develop competencies in creativity, emotional intelligence, critical thinking, and AI fluency to stay relevant.
*   **Future disruptions may expand.** Though physical roles appear safe today, advancements in robotics could change this. This study focuses on language-based AI, not physical automation

Microsoft’s latest research offers a data-driven lens into the future of work one where human insight and technological augmentation must go hand in hand. The message is clear: leverage AI where it adds value but stay agile to future shifts.
