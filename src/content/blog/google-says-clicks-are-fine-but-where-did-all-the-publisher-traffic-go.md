Google Says “Clicks Are Fine” – But Where Did All the Publisher Traffic Go?

Welcome to AI Overviews - Google’s shiny new toy that answers your questions without you needing to click anything. It’s magical for users, disastrous for publishers, and somehow, according to Google, completely harmless.

## “Traffic Is Stable,” Says Google. But Whose Traffic?

In their recent blog [post](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/), Liz Reid from Google made a bold claim:

> **Clicks remain relatively stable. In many cases, we’re sending more quality clicks than before.**

Sounds lovely, right? But here’s what they **didn’t say**:

*   They **shared zero data** to back this.
*   They **didn’t specify** whether traffic went to **publishers**, **YouTube**, **Google Maps**, or **Reddit threads**.
*   They **dismissed third-party traffic data** as unreliable yet refused to publish their own numbers.

Classic PR move: discredit the critics, avoid the audit.

## The Missing Clicks: Where Did They Go?

Let’s assume Google is right that **total outbound clicks** are up. That might even be true if you count:

*   Clicking on a **YouTube video**
*   Tapping a **Reddit link**
*   Visiting a **Google-owned property**
*   Navigating between **Google Flights, Hotels, Shopping** etc.

But for **publishers and websites**, clicks have vanished. And we have data to prove it.

**Source**

**Key Finding**

Ahrefs

Rank-1 CTR drops up to **34.5%** when AI Overview is shown

BrightEdge

CTR dropped **30%**, even as impressions rose **50%**

Pew Research

Only **8%** of sessions with AI Overviews result in _any_ click

MailOnline

CTR from Google fell by **\>50%** on both mobile and desktop

Amsive Digital

Non-branded CTR dropped by nearly **20%**

So where’s the _“higher quality”_ traffic Google keeps talking about?

## “Your Tools Are Wrong” – Google’s Default Excuse

Google’s defense is that **third-party tools** like SimilarWeb, Ahrefs, and BrightEdge “don’t capture the full picture.”

True. Because **Google doesn’t let them**.

In fact, AI Mode often strips away referrer information entirely using `noreferrer` links. That means:

*   Publishers can’t **track traffic** from AI Overviews
*   Analytics tools don’t **attribute the visits correctly**
*   Google Search Console doesn’t **report AI click data separately**

So yes, publishers “don’t see the full picture.” But that’s because **Google broke the window**.

## How the Game Works

Let’s break it down:

**Google’s Claim**

**The Reality**

“Clicks are stable or up”

_Because you now click on Google’s own content or YouTube links_

“Click quality is improving”

_Because the summary gives you the answer - no need to click_

“Publishers are still getting traffic”

_Only if they’re lucky to be cited, and even that barely matters_

“3rd-party data is inaccurate”

_But Google refuses to show its own data_

This is the **zero-click era**. Google gets the engagement. You get the leftovers.

> **Publisher:** Where’s the traffic, then?  
> **Google:** \[Redacted\]

## What Google _Should_ Be Sharing (But Won’t)

If Google wants to be taken seriously in this debate, here’s what they owe us:

1.  **Traffic breakdown by destination**
    *   Publisher websites vs. YouTube vs. Google-owned verticals
2.  **AI Overview engagement data**
    *   How many clicks go to cited sites vs. none at all
3.  **CTR changes with/without AI Overviews**
    *   For each industry: News, Health, Education, Travel, Finance
4.  **Session abandonment rates**
    *   Are users bouncing more because AI already answered their query?

Until then, dismissing third-party data is just a corporate version of “don’t ask questions.”

## Final Word: This Isn’t Just About Traffic

This is about **transparency**, **trust**, and the **future of the open web**.

If AI is the future, we’re all fine with evolving. But don’t kill the ecosystem that trained your models, steal the audience, and then pretend nothing changed.

Because something **did** change. Just ask any publisher looking at their analytics chart. Or rather, trying to figure out where the rest of the chart went.

*   This isn't SEO. It's survival.
