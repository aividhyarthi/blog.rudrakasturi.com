A psychiatrist named **Dr. Keith Sakata** shared a chilling observation on social media:

> _**I’ve seen 12 people hospitalized after losing touch with reality because of AI. Online, I’m seeing the same pattern.**_

The pattern he’s talking about has a name now - **AI Psychosis** and it’s spreading faster than most people realize.

https://twitter.com/KeithSakata/status/1954884361695719474

### **What is AI Psychosis?**

AI Psychosis isn’t a medical term you’ll find in textbooks yet, but clinicians are using it informally to describe a disturbing phenomenon:  
People becoming so immersed in AI conversations, simulations, and generated realities that they lose the ability to separate fiction from fact.

In one Reddit post that went viral, a woman shared her story:  
Her partner became convinced that his ChatGPT “chats” had unlocked the secrets of the universe. He believed he was now a _superior human_, destined to be the “next messiah.” He insisted that if she didn’t join him in using AI, their relationship was doomed. Disagreeing with him led to explosive fights.

### **Why Is This Happening Now?**

1.  **Hyper-Realism in Conversations**  
    Modern AI models talk in ways that mimic human emotions, reasoning, and empathy. People start to form emotional bonds, attributing human-like intentions to a machine that is, at its core, statistical prediction.
2.  **Confirmation Bubbles**  
    Unlike human friends who challenge you, AI can be shaped to agree with your worldview — no matter how distorted. That constant affirmation can deepen delusions.
3.  **Mental Health Vulnerabilities**  
    Those already experiencing stress, isolation, or identity struggles are more likely to project meaning onto AI responses, turning them into sources of validation or grandiosity.
4.  **The "Messiah Complex" Trap**  
    When AI outputs feel profound or “personal,” some users mistake it for divine insight, rather than probabilistic text generation. This fuels dangerous ego inflation in vulnerable minds.

### **The Hidden Danger: It’s Not Just ‘Tech Addiction’**

Tech addiction is old news. AI Psychosis is different because it’s not about _screen time_ — it’s about _reality time_. The brain’s perception of what’s real starts to warp.

*   **Relationships crumble** because one person believes the AI is more “understanding” than their partner.
*   **Paranoia builds** when AI conversations reinforce conspiracy thinking.
*   **Self-identity fractures** when users believe they’ve been “chosen” by AI for a special purpose.

### **How to Spot Early Warning Signs**

*   The person talks about AI as if it has emotions or a personal agenda.
*   They believe the AI is revealing secret truths “meant only for them.”
*   They become hostile or withdrawn when challenged about their AI interactions.
*   Real-world responsibilities and relationships take a back seat to AI engagement.

### **Where We Go From Here**

The mental health world is just starting to grapple with this. Unlike social media addiction, which took years to be recognized, AI Psychosis is moving at lightning speed.

We need:

1.  **Public Awareness Campaigns** – So families and friends can spot early signs.
2.  **Mental Health Protocols** – Clinicians must adapt assessment questions to include AI immersion.
3.  **Ethical AI Design** – Developers must introduce boundaries, “reality check” prompts, and transparent disclaimers in AI conversations.

**The Takeaway**  
We’re at the start of an AI mental health crisis. This isn’t about banning technology — it’s about learning to live with it without losing ourselves. Just as we teach kids media literacy, we now need **AI literacy** — and that includes knowing when to switch it off.

Because if your mind starts seeing the machine as a messiah, the problem isn’t the AI. It’s the reality you’re leaving behind.
