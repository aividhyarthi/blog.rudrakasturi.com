Google Search Console has rolled out a new feature called custom annotations. This update allows website owners and SEO professionals to add short notes directly to the Performance report charts. As per this sources from Google Search Central Blog and Search Engine Journal, the feature is designed to give users clearer insight into why specific traffic patterns or ranking changes occur.

### What Are Custom Annotations in Google Search Console

Custom annotations let you attach short notes to any date within the Performance report. Once added, these notes appear as markers on the chart. As per this sources from Google Search Central Blog, the purpose of this tool is to help users add context to their data without depending on outside documents or complicated tracking systems.

This means when you notice a spike or drop in impressions or clicks, you can immediately see whether a site update, algorithm shift, or content change may have contributed.

### Why Custom Annotations Matter

**1\. Better understanding of performance changes**  
As per this sources from Search Engine Journal, annotations make it easier to identify why traffic moves up or down. You no longer need to rely on memory or guesswork.

**2\. Improved collaboration across teams**  
All users with access to the property can view annotations. This helps SEO teams, content managers, developers, and marketing teams stay aligned.

**3\. More effective SEO reporting**  
Annotations make your reports clearer and more professional. With precise context tied to specific actions, it becomes easier to show clients or managers what influenced performance.

**4\. Saves time and eliminates confusion**  
Instead of digging through emails or project logs to understand changes, the explanation is right inside Search Console.

### How to Add an Annotation

As per this sources from [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/custom-chart-annotations) Blog, the process is simple:

1.  Open your Search Console Performance report.
2.  Right click on the chart on the date you want to annotate.
3.  Select the option to add an annotation.
4.  Write your note within the character limit.
5.  Save it and the marker will appear instantly.

Every user who has access to the property will see the annotation.

### Smart Ways to Use Annotations

Here are common examples of what you should annotate:

*   Publishing or updating content
*   Rolling out a new page design or template
*   Performing technical SEO updates
*   Changing site structure or navigation
*   Correcting indexing issues
*   Launching SEO or marketing campaigns
*   Seasonal or industry related events that affect traffic

As per this sources from Search Engine Journal, Google also recommends marking moments when your content strategy or target search intent shifts.

### Important Limitations to Know

Custom annotations offer great value, but there are a few limitations to keep in mind.

*   You can create up to 200 annotations per property.
*   Annotations automatically delete after 500 days.
*   You cannot edit an existing annotation. You must delete it and recreate it.
*   All users can view annotations, so avoid using sensitive information.
*   The character limit requires you to keep notes short and clear.

As per this sources from Search Engine Land, these limits help ensure annotations remain clean and relevant.

### Best Practices for Using Annotations

**Use them consistently**  
The more events you annotate, the clearer your performance story becomes.

**Keep notes short and descriptive**  
Everyone on the team should understand what the annotation refers to at a glance.

**Integrate annotations into your reporting**  
Use them during monthly SEO reviews to explain why numbers changed.

**Maintain a private backup**  
Since older annotations automatically expire, you may want a separate internal log for long term history.
