In a seismic shift in online behavior, Generation Z is redefining how we search for information, challenging the dominance of search engines like Google. [According to insights reported by Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/software/social-media-new-google/#people_are_more_likely_to_search_for_businesses_on_tiktok_than_instagram_section), a notable decline of 25%, on average, has been observed in the use of Google for search between Gen Z and Gen X. This generational divide signals a significant change in digital habits, raising the question: is social media the new Google?

#### Understanding Generations

To better understand the trends discussed in this article, here’s a breakdown of age groups by generation:

*   **Generation Z (Gen Z):** Born between 1997 and 2012, currently aged 12-27 years.
*   **Millennials:** Born between 1981 and 1996, currently aged 28-43 years.
*   **Generation X (Gen X):** Born between 1965 and 1980, currently aged 44-59 years.
*   **Baby Boomers:** Born between 1946 and 1964, currently aged 60-78 years.

#### Social Media as a Primary Search Tool

A quarter of people (24%) reported, as highlighted in the Forbes article, that they primarily use social media platforms to search online. TikTok, Instagram, and Pinterest have emerged as go-to tools for discovering everything from beauty tutorials to restaurant recommendations and travel guides. TikTok, in particular, has gained traction as a search engine alternative for Gen Z, favored for its visually engaging, quick, and often authentic content.

#### YouTube: The New Google?

Notably, Forbes highlights that 57% of people now search for information on YouTube in the same way they would on Google. Additionally, people are more likely to search for businesses on TikTok than Instagram, underscoring TikTok's growing importance as a search platform. Furthermore, people are most likely to search for "Hair and Makeup" content on TikTok (17%), while Instagram dominates in searches for "Fashion Brands" (12%).

#### Brand Discovery on Social Media

Platforms like Instagram and TikTok not only provide answers but do so in a format that resonates with Gen Z’s preference for bite-sized, visually rich content. This trend is reflected in the daily habits of 24% of people who claim to discover new brands on social media every single day. As Forbes notes, social media has become an ecosystem for brand discovery and engagement, powered by influencers, user-generated content, and targeted advertisements.

#### The Mobile-First Generation

Another striking statistic from Forbes highlights that a third of people (33%) now only use their cellphones to access the internet. For Gen Z, mobile devices are the cornerstone of digital life, aligning perfectly with the design and functionality of social media platforms. This mobile-first behavior underscores the need for brands and businesses to optimize their content for social platforms and ensure a seamless mobile experience.

#### Search Engines Losing Ground to Social Media

While 84% of the population still rely on search engines for brand name searches, a Forbes analysis reveals a 30% decline in search engine use for this purpose between Baby Boomers (94%) and Gen Z (64%) in 2024. This generational gap reflects a broader shift in online behavior, as younger audiences increasingly favor platforms where they can search, interact, and consume information in a single ecosystem.

#### Social Media vs. Traditional Search Habits

Forbes also notes that one in five people don’t feel the need to use search engines because they can access websites through social media. For Gen Z, this shift is particularly pronounced, as they are more likely to use TikTok over Google for various topics. Meanwhile, the topic people are most likely to search for on Google remains "Restaurants and Bars" (81%).

For Baby Boomers, search engines remain the primary gateway to the internet. However, for Gen Z, the internet’s landscape is more fragmented. As Forbes emphasizes, they are more likely to search directly within social media platforms, reflecting a shift toward decentralized search habits.

#### Implications for Businesses and Marketers

This transformation in search behavior has profound implications for businesses. Companies that rely solely on traditional SEO strategies may risk losing a critical segment of younger audiences. Instead, they need to embrace a multi-platform approach:

1.  **Social Media Optimization (SMO):** Just as SEO is essential for search engines, SMO ensures that brands are discoverable within social platforms. Leveraging hashtags, trending topics, and visually appealing content is key.
2.  **Influencer Marketing:** Collaborating with influencers who can authentically showcase your products or services can amplify brand reach and build trust.
3.  **Short-Form Video Content:** Platforms like TikTok thrive on engaging, short videos. Businesses need to invest in creating snackable content that provides value and entertainment.
4.  **Mobile-First Strategies:** With the dominance of mobile usage, ensuring fast-loading pages, mobile-friendly designs, and seamless navigation is no longer optional.

#### The Future of Search

The generational divide in search preferences highlights an evolving digital ecosystem. While Google and other search engines remain critical tools, the rise of social media as a search alternative cannot be ignored. For Gen Z, as Forbes points out, social media offers an interactive, engaging, and visually rich environment that aligns with their digital habits.

As we look ahead, the question isn’t whether social media will replace search engines but how the two will coexist. For businesses, the challenge lies in staying ahead of these trends and meeting audiences where they are—be it on Google, TikTok, or Instagram.

_**Is social media the new Google? For Gen Z, Forbes Digital analysis suggests it certainly seems to be heading in that direction.**_
