Google has started rolling out Tasks in Gemini free of charge in India and five other countries. The update moves Gemini beyond a question-and-answer assistant towards an AI system that can work on multi-step tasks in the background and return with a result.

Google's release notes dated 17 November 2026 name India, the United States, Canada, Australia, Japan and South Korea among the markets included in the rollout. The company says the feature runs on its Hydrogen 4 model and is being made available to hundreds of millions of users. Availability may still arrive in stages, so not every account will necessarily see it at the same time.

## What can Gemini Tasks do?

Google describes tasks such as preparing a morning briefing from email and calendar information, planning a trip and carrying out repetitive research. Users describe the outcome they want, and Gemini works through the steps in the background. Recurring routines are also part of the proposition.

That is different from asking a chatbot to write a checklist. The system is intended to carry out a sequence of actions, then notify the user when the work is ready. The feature is still directed by the user. It should not be treated as permission for an AI system to make important decisions without review.

## Why the India rollout matters

A free tier lowers the barrier to trying agent-style AI. Students could use it to organise research, professionals could prepare routine summaries, and small businesses could explore recurring administrative tasks without immediately buying a separate automation product.

The important question is not how many tasks an AI can claim to automate. It is whether the result is accurate, whether the user can inspect what happened and whether the system asks before taking consequential actions.

## What users should check

1. Open Gemini and look for the Tasks option or task mode. Its appearance may depend on rollout and account availability.
2. Start with a low-risk job, such as collecting information for a travel plan.
3. Give clear boundaries, including the sources to use and what not to do.
4. Check the output and any actions taken before relying on it.
5. Avoid giving an agent access to sensitive accounts or high-impact decisions unless the permissions and safeguards are understood.

## The bigger shift

The move from chat to delegated work is becoming a central battleground for AI assistants. Google is making that experience available to more Indian users at no additional charge, but usefulness will depend on reliability, permissions and transparency, not just the model behind it.

**Bottom line:** Gemini Tasks is rolling out free in India, but treat it as a supervised assistant. Start with repeatable, low-risk work and verify the result.
