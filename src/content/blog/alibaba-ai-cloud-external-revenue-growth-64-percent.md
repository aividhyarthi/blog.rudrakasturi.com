Alibaba's latest financial results provide a useful signal that demand for artificial intelligence is becoming a meaningful cloud business. The company said revenue from external customers for its AI Cloud and Compute Services grew 64% year over year in the quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Alibaba also reported triple-digit growth in AI-related product revenue for the thirteenth consecutive quarter. Cloud adjusted EBITA increased 121%, and the cloud business reported an adjusted EBITA margin of 14.5%.

These figures move the AI conversation beyond model launches and benchmark scores. They show how much customers are spending on the infrastructure and products needed to use AI.

## What do Alibaba's numbers show?

Three indicators stand out.

**External AI cloud demand:** Revenue from external customers for AI Cloud and Compute Services grew 64% year over year. This is an important distinction because it refers to external customer revenue, not all internal and external cloud activity.

**Continued AI product growth:** Alibaba said AI-related product revenue recorded triple-digit growth for the thirteenth consecutive quarter.

**Cloud profitability:** Cloud adjusted EBITA rose 121%, while the adjusted EBITA margin reached 14.5%. Adjusted EBITA is a non-GAAP measure and should be considered alongside the company's full financial statements.

Alibaba's total group revenue grew 10% year over year in the same quarter.

## Why AI cloud revenue is an important metric

A company can announce a new model and attract attention without building a sustainable business. Cloud revenue offers another view of adoption.

When organisations pay for model access, computing capacity, data services and AI products, the technology is moving into operating budgets. That does not automatically mean every AI deployment is profitable for the customer, but it is a stronger commercial signal than free trials or developer interest alone.

## What this means for India

Indian businesses should watch the economics of AI infrastructure closely. The growth of cloud AI services in Asia increases the range of providers available to enterprises, developers and startups.

For Indian companies, the decision should not be based only on model quality. Teams should compare total cost of ownership, data residency, latency, language support, security, service reliability and the ability to move workloads between providers.

A lower model price can still become an expensive deployment if inference volume, integration and data preparation costs are ignored.

## The competition is across the full AI stack

Cloud providers are competing across chips, infrastructure, models, developer tools and enterprise applications. Alibaba's results show why a full-stack strategy matters: revenue can come from compute services as well as products built on top of that infrastructure.

The next question is whether this growth can continue while maintaining margins and competing with other global cloud providers.
