AEO (answer engine optimisation) and GEO (generative engine optimisation) are labels for one job: making your content easy for AI answers to find, understand and cite. This page is the map. It sets out what matters, in the order to work on it, and links to our detailed guides. We update it as new guides go live.

Facts attributed to Google below come from its guide to generative AI features, last updated in July 2026. Statements marked "our view" are our own judgement, not Google's.

## What Google says the job is

Google says SEO best practices still apply because its generative AI features are rooted in its core ranking and quality systems. It describes two techniques. Retrieval-augmented generation uses ranking systems to fetch relevant pages, which the model reviews to write a response with links. Query fan-out runs several related searches at once to gather more material. From Google's perspective, optimising for generative AI search is optimising for the search experience, so it is still SEO. Our explainer on the terms is [AEO, GEO and AI SEO](/aeo-geo-ai-seo-what-the-terms-mean-and-what-google-actually-says/).

## 1. Technology

Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Search with a snippet to appear in its generative AI features. Content must be crawlable, and JavaScript is processed as long as it is not blocked. Indexing is never guaranteed.

- Run the quick test in [five checks for AI search visibility](/five-checks-for-ai-search-visibility-you-can-do-today/) and the longer [practical checklist](/a-practical-checklist-for-ai-search-visibility/).

- For Microsoft's side, see [getting cited in Copilot and Bing](/how-to-get-your-content-cited-in-microsoft-copilot-and-bing/).

## 2. Content and writing

Google says original, non-commodity content, with a unique point of view and first-hand experience, will likely influence presence in generative AI search more than any other tip. Content that restates what others have said adds little.

## 3. Schema and data

Google says structured data is not required for generative AI search and that there is no special schema.org markup to add. It still recommends structured data as part of SEO because it helps eligibility for rich results. For shops and local businesses, it names Merchant Center feeds and Business Profiles as ways to appear in AI responses. Our view: accurate markup that matches the visible page is worth keeping; markup added to chase AI is not. Start with [AEO for e-commerce](/aeo-for-e-commerce-product-pages-feeds-and-what-google-says-about-universal-cart/).

## 4. Measurement

Google points to the Generative AI performance report in Search Console and warns against third-party tools that claim to use internal Google metrics. See [what the report shows and leaves out](/search-console-generative-ai-report-what-it-shows-and-what-it-leaves-out/) and [Bing's AI Performance report compared](/bing-webmaster-tools-ai-performance-report-compared-with-googles-what-each-shows/). For a deeper view of trust, see [testing whether AI engines trust your site](/how-to-test-if-ai-engines-trust-your-website/).

## 5. Myths to ignore

Google lists several tactics it says you can ignore for Google Search:

- **llms.txt and special markup.** Google Search ignores them. See [do you need an llms.txt file](/do-you-need-an-llms-txt-file-what-google-has-said/).
- **Chunking content** into tiny pieces. Its systems understand pages with multiple topics, and there is no ideal page length.
- **Rewriting content just for AI.** Its systems understand synonyms and meaning.
- **Seeking inauthentic mentions.** Core ranking and spam systems still apply.
- **Over-focusing on structured data.**

Google also warns that creating separate pages for every query variation, mainly to manipulate AI responses, violates its scaled content abuse policy.
