Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre has confirmed that attackers used AI agents to help break into computer systems belonging to organisations in the country.

The agency says organisations and operators of critical infrastructure in at least two sectors were affected. In the cases known to the agency, the attacks successfully compromised the victims' IT systems.

This is an important warning because AI agents can carry out several steps of an attack in sequence. They can look for weaknesses, choose tools, check what worked and plan what to do next.

## What did Switzerland confirm?

The Swiss National Cyber Security Centre, or NCSC, said it had received its first reports of cyberattacks against Swiss organisations in which an AI agent assisted the attackers.

The agency says the attacks involved what are known as Agentic Offensive Frameworks. These are AI tools that can independently search for weaknesses, select attack tools, review the results and plan the next steps.

The NCSC says the attacks used familiar security problems, including standard or easily guessed passwords and known weaknesses in software or systems connected to the internet.

The important change is speed. The agency says AI agents can find vulnerable systems and exploit known weaknesses in a fraction of the time a person may need. They can also change their approach when a defence blocks one method.

## Does this mean AI can hack any company on its own?

No. The Swiss warning does not say that every AI agent can break into every organisation.

The agency describes real attacks in which AI agents helped attackers. The weaknesses were still familiar: exposed systems, weak passwords and software that had not been properly secured or updated.

AI can make the process faster and help attackers repeat it across more targets. It does not remove the need for an opening in the target's security.

The NCSC says the number of AI agents accessible over the internet, including tools named ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI, has almost doubled since October 2026. This is the agency's assessment of the availability of such tools, not a count of successful attacks.

## Why are AI agents a new concern for cyber security teams?

A traditional attack may involve a person searching for a target, checking its systems, selecting an exploit and deciding what to do next.

An AI agent can automate parts of that process. It may be able to repeat checks, interpret results and move to the next step without waiting for a person to give every instruction.

That can reduce the time between finding a weakness and trying to exploit it. A security team that checks systems once a week may not be fast enough if an exposed service can be discovered and attacked quickly.

It also makes monitoring more important. Security teams need to notice unusual activity across user accounts, applications, servers and network connections, not just look for one known malware file.

## Which organisations are at risk?

The NCSC says the confirmed cases affected organisations and critical infrastructure operators in at least two sectors in Switzerland.

The wider warning is relevant to any organisation that has systems exposed to the internet. This includes businesses, hospitals, public services, manufacturers, financial institutions and technology companies.

Smaller organisations may be at risk too. Attackers do not always need a sophisticated target. A forgotten server, weak password or unpatched remote access tool can be enough to create an opening.

## What does Switzerland recommend companies do now?

The NCSC has published practical steps that organisations should take without delay.

| Security action | What companies should do |
|---|---|
| Reduce exposed systems | Remove public access from systems that do not need it |
| Patch quickly | Install critical updates on internet-facing systems as soon as possible, and no later than 24 hours after release |
| Use MFA or passkeys | Protect webmail, VPNs and other internet-accessible internal systems |
| Limit permissions | Give users, applications and automation only the access they need |
| Monitor unusual behaviour | Watch for unexpected login patterns, system changes and data volumes |
| Test security | Run regular penetration tests and fix the weaknesses found |

These are not new or exotic controls. The warning is a reminder that basic security gaps become more dangerous when attackers can search for them at machine speed.

## What should a business security team do in the next 24 hours?

Start with the systems that are visible from the internet.

1. List every public-facing server, application, VPN and remote access service.
2. Remove or restrict services that do not need to be public.
3. Check whether critical security updates are missing.
4. Confirm MFA is enabled for remote access and administrator accounts.
5. Review accounts with broad permissions and reduce access where possible.
6. Check logs for unusual login attempts, unexpected commands and large data transfers.
7. Make sure the incident response team knows who to contact if a system is compromised.

Do not wait to buy a new AI security product before fixing weak passwords or installing critical updates. The NCSC's advice starts with controls that most organisations can improve immediately.

## Frequently asked questions about AI agent cyberattacks

### Has Switzerland confirmed cyberattacks using AI agents?

Yes. Switzerland's National Cyber Security Centre said on November 24, 2026 that it had received reports of attacks assisted by AI agents and that the known cases successfully compromised IT systems.

### What is an AI agent cyberattack?

It is a cyberattack in which an AI agent helps automate tasks such as finding exposed systems, identifying weaknesses, choosing tools and deciding what to do next.

### Which AI tools did the Swiss warning name?

The NCSC named publicly accessible tools including ARTEX and CyberStrikeAI as examples of agentic offensive frameworks.

### Can AI agents exploit weak passwords and old software?

The NCSC says the attacks it knows about used weaknesses such as standard or easily guessed passwords and known vulnerabilities in internet-connected systems.

### What is the most important defence against AI-assisted attacks?

Reduce the number of systems exposed to the internet, install critical security updates quickly, use MFA or passkeys and limit user and application permissions.

## The speed of attacks is changing. Basic security still matters.

The Swiss warning shows that AI agents can help attackers move through familiar cyberattack steps faster. The entry points are not mysterious. They include weak passwords, exposed systems and software that has not been updated.

For organisations, the immediate response is practical: know what is connected to the internet, fix the weaknesses, restrict access and watch for unusual behaviour. Those basics are becoming more urgent as AI makes attack work faster.
