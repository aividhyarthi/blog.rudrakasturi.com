You have probably seen three new terms this year: **AEO**, **GEO** and **AI SEO**. They sound like three new skills. Here is what each one means.

## What the terms mean

- **AEO (answer engine optimization):** making your content easy for tools that give *answers* (Google's AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity) to find and use.
- **GEO (generative engine optimization):** almost the same idea. It is about being mentioned or cited when an AI writes an answer.
- **AI SEO:** a catch-all label for doing SEO with AI search in mind.

## What Google says

In mid-May 2026 Google published a guide called "Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search". Its main message, as reported by Search Engine Journal and Search Engine Land: AEO and GEO are **not separate things**. Optimizing for AI search is still SEO.

The guide also lists some myths. According to the coverage, Google says you do **not** need:

- an llms.txt file (we explain this in [our llms.txt article](/do-you-need-an-llms-txt-file-what-google-has-said/));
- special markup or special schema just for AI features;
- to chop your content into tiny pieces for AI systems.

What Google says to focus on instead:

- **Content that adds something new.** Google calls it "non-commodity content": insight beyond what everyone already knows.
- **The technical basics:** pages that can be crawled, clean HTML, JavaScript done properly, good page experience, and less duplicate content.
- **For shops and local businesses:** keep your Merchant Center feeds and Google Business Profile up to date.

Danny Sullivan said something similar earlier: good SEO is good GEO, and great content for people works for AI search too.

## The other side of the argument

Not everyone agrees Google's guide tells the whole story. Search Engine Land published a commentary calling it "naive and self-serving". Its point: AI tools also lean on what the rest of the web says about you, such as Wikipedia, Reddit and other publications. So being **mentioned elsewhere** can matter, not only what is on your own site.

Also, Google's guide is about Google. ChatGPT and Perplexity may weigh things differently, and the guide does not settle that.

## What this means for you

1. Do not buy a new "AEO service" before the basics are solid. Crawling, clear writing and original insight come first.
2. Write answers clearly, but do not chop pages into robot-sized bits.
3. Be useful enough that other sites and communities talk about you.
4. Treat advice from tool vendors as ideas to test, not rules.
