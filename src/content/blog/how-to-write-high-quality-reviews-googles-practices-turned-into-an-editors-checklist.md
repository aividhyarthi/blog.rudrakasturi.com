"Best of" pages and reviews are a crowded category, and thin ones are easy to spot. Google's review guidance (last updated 10 December 2025 as we read it) lists what separates a useful review from a summary.

**Best for:** review editors, affiliate sites, publishers and ecommerce content teams.

![Review checklist: show expertise, add first-hand evidence, measure and compare, be honest about trade-offs](/images/write-high-quality-reviews/review-checklist.svg)

## What Google says

Reviews can cover products, services, destinations, games, movies and other topics, and can come from an expert staff member or merchant, an independent blogger or a publisher's editorial team. Google's practices include:

- Evaluate from a user's perspective, and show that you are knowledgeable about what you review.
- Provide evidence such as visuals, audio or links showing your own experience, to support your expertise and the authenticity of the review.
- Share quantitative measurements of how something performs in various categories.
- Explain what sets something apart from competitors, and cover comparable things to consider or which suits which circumstances.
- Discuss benefits and drawbacks based on your own original research.
- Describe how a product has evolved from previous models, and focus on the most important decision-making factors.
- Describe key design choices and their effect on users beyond what the manufacturer says.
- Include links to other useful resources, and consider links to multiple sellers.
- When recommending something as best overall or for a purpose, say why, with first-hand supporting evidence.
- Make ranked lists stand on their own even if you also write single in-depth reviews.
- If you use affiliate links, see Google's position on affiliate programs.
- Focus on quality and originality, not length.

## What to do: editor's scoring sheet

Score each review 0 to 2 per row. The scoring is ours.

| Row | Evidence to record |
|---|---|
| Expertise visible | Author page, relevant background |
| Own evidence | Photos, audio, test notes, dates |
| Measurements | At least two numbers from your own testing |
| Comparison | Named alternatives and who each suits |
| Trade-offs | At least two drawbacks from your testing |
| Decision factors | The two or three that matter most |
| Where to buy | More than one seller, if relevant |
| Ranked list | Each pick explains why it is best |

A total under 10 of 16 goes back for another round.

## What to do: step by step

| Step | Owner | Time | How to check | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Plan the testing | Reviewer | 1 hour | A checklist of measurements | Tests are defined before buying |
| 2. Capture evidence as you go | Reviewer | During testing | Dated photos, notes, files | A folder per product |
| 3. Draft with trade-offs | Writer | 3 hours | Each pro has a con | No review is all praise |
| 4. Run the scoring sheet | Editor | 20 min | Score each row with evidence | Total is 10 or more |
| 5. Check links and disclosure | Editor | 15 min | Affiliate links labelled as sponsored | See our link labelling guide |
| 6. Revisit when products change | Editor | Quarterly | Dated update notes | Review shows an honest date |

## Common mistakes

- Rewriting manufacturer specifications as a review.
- Ranking products you did not test.
- Using length as a substitute for evidence.
- Hiding affiliate relationships. See [rel sponsored, ugc and nofollow](/rel-sponsored-ugc-and-nofollow-how-to-qualify-outbound-links-with-a-decision-table/).
